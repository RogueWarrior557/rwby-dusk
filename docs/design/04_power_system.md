# Hushlight: the power system (the Lumen Arts)

**Status:** canonical (v1, 2026-11-23). It extends `01_narrative_bible.md`
§2–3. If anything conflicts, this document wins on how power works, and the
bible wins on plot.

**Why this exists.** Review found two gaps:
1. The world had a *cost* (burning memory) but no real *magic system*: no
   variety of sources, disciplines or mysteries to explain healers,
   mages and fighters.
2. The protagonist had no exceptional power.

This document gives the world a deep, rule-bound magic with factions,
forbidden arts, legendary artifacts and a lost art, and it makes Maren the
most powerful fighter in the game.

---

## 1. The core idea in three sentences
1. All power in the Reach is **lumen**, the living light of remembrance.
2. Memory is only the most *common* source of lumen. There are **four
   others**: fallen sunlight (Vigilglass), living sun-fire (Sunseed),
   being remembered by others (Renown) and the Hush itself (unmemory, the
   anti-light).
3. People shape lumen through **seven Arts**, each with its own cost, culture
   and faction. An eighth Art, **Naming**, was lost when Myrr was forgotten.

---

## 2. The five sources of power
| Source | What it is | Who has access | Its cost or limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Memory** (inner lumen) | Every person's remembered life glows faintly. Stronger memories (love, grief, terror) glow brighter. | Everyone, though most people have only a little to spare | Using it destructively (burning) loses the memory forever |
| **Vigilglass** (stored lumen) | Crystallised sunlight that fell in the Glass Wastes during the Long Night of Orra | Church, scholars, the rich | Finite. Cracks with use and is mined at deadly risk. **Focuses** power, never creates it. |
| **Sunseed** (living fire) | Nine coals of the Vigil's own fire that fell burning at the Dimming and have never gone out. Each Ember-folk clan keeps one in its hearth. | The Ember-folk only | Only nine exist. A Sunseed burns only while a story is being told to it. |
| **Renown** (outer lumen) | Being remembered *by others* feeds you lumen. Heroes of told stories grow brighter; the forgotten fade. This is the hidden law behind the Unremembered and behind restoration. | Anyone people talk about, which makes legends genuinely powerful | Fickle. Renown follows what people believe, not what is true. |
| **The Hush** (unmemory) | The anti-light: absence and silence. It can be drawn on as power: to be unseen, to silence, to unmake. | Anyone willing to pay | **The user is slowly forgotten.** Use too much and even your friends lose your name. |

**Why this matters for intrigue:** every source is controlled, contested or
forbidden. That powers the politics, the heists, the factions and the
mysteries.

## 3. The seven Arts (and the lost eighth)

### 3.1 Kindling: the art of burning
* **What it does:** burns memory (or recall-oil) as **true light**: light,
  heat and concussive force. Lamplighters, Church Wick-Knights and every
  city lamp use it.
* **Rule:** power equals the memory's emotional weight times the burner's
  lumen density. A stranger's childhood summer lights a street for a week.
  A **Wick-born's** single memory can light a city for a night.
* **Cost:** the memory is gone forever. In truth it also unravels the
  world (the Secret).
* **Forbidden branch: Draughting.** *Drinking* recall instead of burning it.
  The drinker gains a surge of lumen *and* lives the stranger's memory for a
  while. It is addictive and identity-blurring, and illegal everywhere.
  Cass the Moth secretly does it.
* **Industrial branch: Distillation.** Extracting memories into oil. House
  monopoly.
* **Controlled by:** the Lantern Houses (fuel), the Church (licences, the
  Last Rite), the Order (Old Lanterns).

### 3.2 Oathbinding: the art of holding
* **What it does:** swear an oath upon a memory and it becomes an
  **anchor**. Anchors grant resilience, strength and an unbreakable will.
  An oathbound knight can stand in a Hush-tide that would strip an ordinary
  person bare.
* **Rule:** each anchor must be a memory you truly cherish. The more anchors
  you hold, the harder you are to break. Bram's perfect memory gives him
  more anchors than any person alive, which is why he is the toughest
  shield in the Reach.
* **Cost:** an anchored memory can never be burned, and **breaking the oath
  shatters the memory**. It is lost the way burning loses it.
* **Practised by:** the Order's knights, House guards, Ember-folk
  champions. Ines, Bram, Ruun.

### 3.3 The Telling: the art of sharing
* **What it does:** a memory given to another person, by speaking, singing
  or writing, kindles lumen in the listener **without consuming the
  original**. It is the only art that creates power from nothing but
  attention. Its branches:
  * **Mending** (healing). *The body remembers being whole.* A Mender
    retells the flesh its uninjured shape, and wounds close. The Mender must
    *hold* the memory of the wound while it heals, so they feel the pain. A
    Keeper-Mender (Tamsin) takes it into himself; a Story-Mender (Kesh)
    spreads it thin across many listeners.
  * **Hymn** (voice). Song carries memory to many listeners at once. It
    inspires allies and swells existing light. Lucien.
  * **Inscription** (writing). What is written stays remembered. A
    name in a ledger cannot slip into the Hush, so marked enemies stay
    revealed. Hester.
  * **Renown** (performance). Drawing on the attention of onlookers. The
    more eyes on you, the brighter you burn. Vesper.
* **Cost:** time, presence and exhaustion. Menders literally share the hurt.
* **Status:** the Church licenses Mending and Hymn and treats the rest as
  folk magic. The Ember-folk practise the oldest, purest form.

### 3.4 Lenscraft: the art of focusing
* **What it does:** shapes lumen through Vigilglass. Beams, mirrors, wards,
  scrying lenses. The Sun-Altar at Aurel is the greatest lens ever built.
* **Rule:** glass multiplies and directs light; it never makes its own. A
  lens without a light source is just a pretty stone.
* **Cost:** glass cracks under strain. Good lenses are irreplaceable.
* **Practised by:** Glass Wastes scholars and Church engineers. Isolde,
  Otto.

### 3.5 Storyfire: the art of the Sunseed
* **What it does:** fire lit from a Sunseed coal and **fed by a Telling**.
  Storyfire "remembers" what it's told: arrows that follow their target,
  flames that burn only the named enemy, braziers that can't be quenched
  while the tale continues.
* **Rule:** Storyfire lasts exactly as long as the story told to it. The
  Ember-folk tell their clan's history to their Sunseed every night. **This
  is why Sefa's sold Telling is so catastrophic:** the Ninth Fire's coal is
  dimming as its verses are forgotten.
* **Cost:** a living teller and a coal. There are nine coals in the world.
* **Practised by:** the Ember-folk. Sefa (who carries **a stolen spark of the
  Ninth Fire's Sunseed** in her braid beads), Ruun.

### 3.6 Hushcraft: the art of the hollow
* **What it does:** draws on unmemory.
  * **Slip:** for a moment, the people around you cannot remember you are
    standing there. Their eyes slide off you. This is real stealth.
  * **Hush:** silence a voice, a spell or a flame.
  * **Dread:** make an enemy forget why it was brave.
  * **Unmake:** make a wound, a trail or a fact briefly forgotten by the
    world.
* **Rule:** you borrow from the Hush, and it collects. Every use makes the
  world remember you a little less.
* **Cost:** **you are forgotten.** Nan Corvane traded her own name to master
  it. Nox's friends sometimes forget her for a heartbeat when she's
  standing beside them.
* **Status:** **forbidden** by the Church and feared by everyone. The Quiet
  embraces it.
* **Practised by:** Nox, Nan, Juniper, Kasimir, Wynn (a little), and every
  Unremembered by nature: the Nameless Knight, Tobin.

### 3.7 Wickwork: the art of artifice
* **What it does:** machines powered by recall-oil, glass slivers or wound
  springs. Flare-muskets, clockwork lantern-walkers, alchemical flasks,
  lock-breakers.
* **Rule:** a machine is only as good as its fuel and its maker.
* **Cost:** fuel (which usually means somebody's memory), and Church
  licensing.
* **Practised by:** tinkers, the Watch, distillers. Pell (who runs his
  Wicklings on glass slivers so they burn nobody's memory), Silas, Pim.

### 3.8 Naming: the lost art of Myrr
* **What it does:** speak a being's **true name**, its whole remembrance, with
  lumen behind it, and you can **bind** it, **restore** it from the Hush, or
  **unmake** it entirely.
* **History:** Myrr's bell-wardens practised it. Every citizen had a
  name-bell. **King Oswy's Unmourning was Naming used as an atrocity:** he
  un-named 40,000 war-dead at once, which wounded the Witness and started
  the Dimming. The backlash erased Myrr itself. The art was lost, and only
  Ilka preserves fragments.
* **Rule:** to Name something you must hold the full weight of its memory,
  joy and grief together. This is why it is so rare, and so dangerous.
* **The secret:** Saint Idony's recall was copied from Oswy's notes. Kindling
  is a crippled, wasteful shadow of Naming.
* **Who can learn it:** in theory, only a **Wick-born** who carries a true
  name inside them. In the whole game, only **Maren** does.

## 9. Maren Vey, rebuilt: the face of the game

### 9.1 What makes her special (five layers of power)
1. **Wick-born.** The rarest gift in the Reach, about one per century. Her
   lumen burns like a bonfire beside candles. A single memory of hers can
   light a city for a night. This is why Ansel chose her, and why everyone
   now wants her.
2. **Bearer of the First Lantern.** The legendary lantern of the Order's
   founders. It is forged from Myrr's bell-bronze and the lens of the Lethe
   Engine, and it is the only lantern that can hold a Wick-born's flame. Its
   hooked pole is a **lantern-glaive**, a real weapon. **It is also the only
   key to the Sun-Altar,** which puts her at the centre of every faction's
   plans.
3. **The Greywater Flame.** On the Night of the Long Tide, at 18, she held
   the Greywater causeway **alone, all night**, against a Hush-tide, and
   saved more than 300 travellers. Ballads are sung about her in every
   ferry town. Her **Renown** makes her brighter still. **The irony at the
   heart of the story:** the light that made her a legend was her brother,
   burned.
4. **Lantern-Captain.** The Order made her its youngest Captain in ninety
   years. She commands her own company (the player's roster) and the right to
   requisition any lantern on the roads. Ines thinks she didn't earn it.
5. **The first Namer in 312 years** (Act III). Six years of Tobin's
   wick-ash inside a lantern built from the Lethe Engine's lens have marked
   her. In Myrr she awakens **Naming**: she can restore the forgotten, unmake
   what must end, and **make light without burning**. This is the power that
   decides the world's fate.

### 9.2 Who she is (no longer a blank canvas)
* **Personality.** Bold, wry and commanding. Reckless when people she's
  responsible for are in danger. She leads from the front, and soldiers
  follow her because she is always the first one into the dark. She jokes
  in a crisis and goes silent after one.
* **Flaw.** She is proud of a legend she doesn't understand. She treats
  burning as a tool she's entitled to: "they're my memories". She keeps
  everyone at arm's length because she has no past to share.
* **What she wants.** To be worthy of the legend, and to keep every person
  on her roads alive.
* **What she fears.** That she's nothing but the light, a lantern with no one
  inside.
* **Voice:** *"Stay behind me. If the dark wants you, it can come through
  me."*

### 9.3 How she plays (combat redesign)
**Change from the proposal:** Maren is no longer a passive Hearth
commander. She is a **deployable frontline hero and the strongest unit at
launch**, always fielded as the company's Captain. The base object becomes
**the Beacon**, a planted lantern-post that is still the run's health, as
in the proposal.
| Element | Design |
|---|---|
| Role | **Lamplighter** (unique): a frontline striker-guardian |
| Weapon | **Lantern-glaive**: sweeping arcs of burning light that hit everything in front of her |
| Passive: *Beacon-bearer* | Her First Lantern is a **large light source that moves with her** (radius 2). Where Maren goes, the light goes. Every positioning decision orbits her. |
| Skill: *Wickflare* (auto) | Hurls a lance of lantern-fire down a line, damaging and **revealing** veiled enemies |
| Ultimate: *Last Ember* (player-triggered) | Burns a fragment of memory: a **blinding detonation** that clears darkness from a wide area, stuns every enemy in it and deals massive damage. Once per Night. |
| Progression, two paths mirroring the story | **Ember Path** (unlocked by burn choices): bigger, more destructive Last Embers, at narrative cost. **Remembrance Path** (unlocked by memories kept and bonds): Telling-like auras that strengthen allies near her, leading to **Naming** in Act III. Both are strong, and they play differently. The player's story choices shape her fighting style. |
| Awakened form (Act III) | **The Unburnt Flame**: her lantern burns with no fuel. She can **Name** a fallen ally back into the fight once per Night, and **un-name** an elite or boss phase. |

### 9.4 The plot changes this power creates
* **Act I:** factions start circling her lantern. Tallis's hunters at
  Greywater are after Bram *and* the First Lantern. Isolde's coded reports
  to Aurelie include measurements of the lantern's output.
* **Act II (the big new dilemma):** Aurelie learns Maren is Wick-born and
  makes an offer. *"You alone could fuel the Kindling. Give yourself to the
  Altar and I will spare all 30,000."* Maren must answer the question she
  has dodged all game: what is she willing to burn when it's **all of
  her**? This ties to the hidden Lantern-Bearer ending, where she does burn
  everything, but for the Witness and the truth, not for Aurelie's lie.
* **Act II:** Vesper's betrayal order (steal the lantern) now has huge
  stakes. The lantern is the key to the Kindling. If he steals it, the
  company must fight to win it back before the Longest Night.
* **Act III:** in Myrr, Maren learns what her lantern is made from (the
  Lethe Engine's lens) and that **the same art that destroyed Myrr is
  inside her**. The question changes from "can I save the world?" to
  "should anyone hold power like this?"
