## 1. Narrative direction on one page

**Logline.** In a world where the only light that holds back the dark is
made by burning memories, a Lamplighter who has burned away half her own
past discovers that the burning is killing the sun, and that she once
burned her own brother out of existence.

**Tone: cartoon surface, serious heart.** Warmth and humour on the surface;
grief, exploitation and identity taken seriously underneath. The rules:
* Darkness lives in faces and situations, never in gore. Unremembered fade
  into motes.
* Every comic character has a wound; every grim character has one thing
  that makes them laugh.
* No villain is wrong about everything.
* Hope is earned through remembering, never handed out.

**Themes:** memory is identity; the cost of keeping going; grief versus
forgetting; exploitation (the poor's past lights the rich's present); what
the living owe the dead.

**Central dramatic question:** *should the world keep burning its past to
survive, or carry its past and risk the dark?*

**Act themes and Maren's active goal**
| Act | Theme | Maren's goal | Ends when |
|---|---|---|---|
| I: The Greywater Road | **What light costs** | Keep her road lit and get her people to Candlemere | She learns her mentor leads the Quiet and her friend betrayed her |
| II: Candlemere | **Who pays** | Expose the truth and stop the Great Kindling | The Kindling's False Dawn proves the secret, and the road leads into the Hush |
| III: Myrr | **What we owe the dead** | Find what the Witness is, and decide the world's answer | The final choice at the Sun-Altar |

## 2. Cosmology: what the world believes and what is true
| Layer | What people believe | What is true |
|---|---|---|
| The Witness | A god who remembered the world into being | The **collective memory of everyone who has ever died**, held together by the living's mourning. It "remembered the world" because the dead remember the places and people they loved. |
| The Vigil (sun) | The Witness's eye | The Witness's light: the brightness of all that collective remembrance |
| The Dimming | Divine punishment (Church); entropy (scholars); grief (Ember-folk) | A **wound**. At the Unmourning of Myrr (0 R.D.), King Oswy forcibly erased 40,000 war-dead from every living mind. The Witness lost them at once, and the Vigil faltered. |
| Recall | A gift of Saint Idony | Idony reverse-engineered **King Oswy's Lethe engine** from notes found in a forgotten library. Every burned memory is a strand of the world's remembrance consumed: a small Unmourning. |
| The Last Rite tithe | Feeding the faithful dead to the Vigil | Burning the dead's last memories **starves the Witness directly**. The Dimming's acceleration since 163 R.D. matches the tithe. |
| The Unremembered | Monsters of the dark | People, places and things whose remembrance fell below a threshold. They hunger for one thing: their names. |
| The Hush | Evil dark | Unmemory. Not malicious: it is what is left when nothing remembers. |

## 3. The rules of light (canonical)
1. **True light** comes only from recall or from Vigilglass. Ordinary fire
   frightens the weakest Unremembered and stops nothing.
2. **A burned memory is gone** from the one who held it.
3. **The threshold of unremembering.** When the last strong memory of a
   person or place is burned, its hold on the world fails. It becomes
   Unremembered, and other people's weaker memories of it fade too, like
   ink in rain. **Only extraordinary minds hold on**: Bram's perfect memory
   keeps a slippery impression.
4. **An Old Lantern burns only its bearer.** The bearer chooses the memory.
   A lantern can be passed to a new bearer by a willing hand.
5. **Wick-ash.** Every Old Lantern keeps the ash of what it has burned.
   Ash is not memory, but it holds the *shape* of one: enough to guide a
   restoration if other rememberers supply the rest.
6. **The Telling.** A memory spoken aloud kindles faint lumen in the
   listeners and **costs nothing**. Many people telling the same name can
   lift an Unremembered back over the threshold. It is slow and fragile,
   and it requires someone to carry grief instead of burning it.
7. **Vigilglass** (Glass Wastes, the Sun-Altar) **focuses** light; it does
   not create it. The Church believes the Sun-Altar channels burned memory
   *into* the Vigil. In truth it borrows tomorrow's light for today: a
   False Dawn followed by a faster fall.

## 5. The Lantern Reach: regions and daily life
The regions are as in v1:
* the Greywater Road;
* Saltreach and the Drowned Coast;
* the Glass Wastes;
* Candlemere;
* the Ashen Marches;
* Aurel;
* Myrr.

Daily life details also stand as in v1: the Grey Hour, the slate-board
memory prices, Last Rites, lantern law, and the children's rhyme
(*"Burn a name, lose a lane…"*). v2 adds two things:
* **Name-bells.** The old custom (from Myrr, though nobody knows it) of
  hanging a small bell for each dead family member survives in the Ember
  Marches.
* **Memory-tells.** Small tin tokens the poor buy to remind themselves
  what they sold. They are useless, and sold by the Houses.

## 7. The ticking clock
* **Chapter 3:** Otto's measurements say the Vigil fails in **seven
  winters** at the current rate of burning.
* **Chapter 4:** Aurelie announces the Kindling for **the Longest Night**,
  forty days away.
* **End of Act II:** the partial Kindling causes a **False Dawn**: a week
  of real daylight, and the Reach rejoices. Then the Grey Hour shrinks to
  minutes. Otto's new estimate is **one winter**. Act III runs against
  that clock.

## 8. The main story (Prologue and 12 chapters)
Each chapter is 5–8 Nights (battles) plus Vigil scenes, and takes 60–90
minutes on mobile.

### Prologue: "The Last Ferry"
Maren escorts a lantern caravan across the Greywater causeway as a
Hush-tide arrives early (tutorial). She burns a small memory, a breakfast,
to finish the Night: the cost shown at no real stake. **Plant:** the
caravan ledger lists *"Lamplighter Vey and one minor"* from six years ago.
Maren has always travelled alone. She shrugs it off as a clerk's error.

### ACT I: THE GREYWATER ROAD (What light costs)

**Ch 1: "The Lamp at Greywater"** (the vertical slice)
* **Goal:** relight Greywater's beacon before the Hush-tide.
* **Synopsis.** Greywater sold its own history for oil a generation ago,
  and the town is fading. Maren finds:
  * **Bram**, hiding from Tallis hunters, who says *"You came through here
    six years ago. With a boy."*;
  * **Sefa**, who refuses to stand in lantern-light;
  * **Brother Tamsin**, carrying a Church writ to seize the recall stores,
    who frowns as if he has met Maren before;
  * **Nox**, halfway through stealing those same stores.
  The tide forces them together.
* **Boss: the Ferryman.** Greywater's forgotten founder. He snuffs the
  nearest lantern unless someone in the light speaks his name. Bram
  remembers it from a carving he once saw, and the first Telling breaks
  the boss.
* **Choice (Burn #1):** burn a memory to relight the beacon (the town
  survives), or evacuate and let Greywater go dark.
* **Final frame:** a hooded boy with a grey smudge for a face, at the edge
  of the light: *"Sister."*

**Ch 2: "Candles for the Drowned"**
* **Goal:** keep the coast road open to Saltreach.
* **Synopsis:**
  * **Wynn** (deaf eel-poacher) and **Nan Corvane** (the Hushwife who
    raised her) guide the company.
  * **Pell**, the lamp-maker, joins at a waystation, along with his
    Wicklings.
  * A distillery village, **Cinderwick**, is losing streets: doors open
    onto fog.
  * A tall knight in rust-black plate helps hold a causeway, says *"I knew
    this road once,"* and is gone by dawn (**the Nameless Knight**,
    cameo).
* **Boss: the Tidewife.** The collective Unremembered of Lowmere, Wynn's
  drowned village. Her mother is in it.
* **Choice:**
  * **Free** the drowned: Wynn signs their names while Nan speaks them.
    The fight is harder, Wynn's bond rises, and Nan reveals she herself is
    fading.
  * **Seal** them with Pell's lantern-ward: safer, but Wynn is hurt, and
    the Tidewife returns hostile in Act III.
* **Plant:** Nan: *"Your lantern keeps sniffing at the same door, dearie.
  Something in its ash wants out."*

**Ch 3: "The Glass Road"**
* **Goal:** escort the observatory's data to Candlemere.
* **Synopsis:**
  * In the Glass Wastes, **Otto** shows her measurements: the Vigil fails
    in **seven winters**, and it fell faster after 163 R.D.
  * Her estranged protégé **Isolde** joins the company, warm and brilliant.
  * **Ines Calder** arrives as an Order envoy to summon Maren to an
    inquiry over "irregular use of the Marrow lantern". She resents Maren
    for inheriting it.
* **Boss: the Glass Colossus.** A forgotten statue of Saint Idony,
  walking.
* **Choice:**
  * **Share** Otto's data with the Order through Ines: Ines respects Maren
    sooner, but the data reaches the Church faster.
  * **Keep** it secret: Isolde will have to steal it, making her betrayal
    personal.
* **Plants:**
  * Otto's ledger holds a line in Idony's hand: *"The light is borrowed."*
  * Isolde's coded letters appear in the Archive for observant players.

**Ch 4: "The Lighthouse at Candlemere"**
* **Goal:** reach Candlemere and answer the inquiry.
* **Synopsis.** New allies:
  * **Hester Quill**, the disgraced ledger-keeper, who stares when she
    hears the name "Castell";
  * **Vesper Hale**, a House duelist who flirts with everyone except Ines;
  * **Constable Pim**, who arrests Sefa within the hour;
  * **the Moth**, a vigilante robbing distilleries;
  * **Ines**, who joins under orders.

  Aurelie announces **the Great Kindling** on the Longest Night. The Quiet
  attacks the **Lighthouse Spire**, which burns condemned prisoners'
  memories. Its guardian **Kasimir** holds the gate. Its leader is
  unmasked: **Ansel Marrow**, alive. **Isolde betrays** the company,
  handing Otto's data and Tamsin's heresy to Aurelie, and Tamsin is
  arrested.
* **Boss: Kasimir, the Unlit Wall** (at the Spire gate).
* **Choice (Burn #2):**
  * **Defend** the Spire: burn a memory to power Last Ember. The city stays
    lit; that night's prisoners still burn.
  * **Let it fall:** prisoners freed, a night of riots, and Maren is
    blamed.
* **Act I ending:** Ansel, gently: *"What did your light ever cost you? You
  burned it so you wouldn't have to carry it."* As he leaves, the grey-faced
  boy takes his hand.

### ACT II: CANDLEMERE (Who pays)

**Ch 5: "The Undercity Still"**
* **Goal:** find proof the Church can't bury.
* **Synopsis:**
  * The company is hunted and goes underground. They meet **Silas Vash**,
    Idony's last descendant, who knows the testament is locked in
    **House Tallis's memory vault**, bought from the Church as leverage.
  * In a forgotten Order chapel the company finds the **Nameless Knight**,
    sleeping beside a statue whose name plate has been chiselled off. He
    joins.
  * **Heist:** Cass, Nox and Hester break the vault. They recover the
    **testament** and Hester's **missing ledgers**, which record House
    Tallis buying children's memories, among them "N. Tallis, aged 14,
    sold as Castell".
* **Boss: the Residue.** Born from burned memory in the vats; Bram has seen
  it before.
* **Reveal:** *"The light is borrowed… I have taught them to eat the house
  to warm the hearth."*
* **Choice:**
  * **Publish** the testament now: panic, a lower-ward uprising, and the
    Church cracks down (Ch 6 is harder).
  * **Hold** it as leverage for the Longest Night.

**Ch 6: "The Choir and the Cell"**
* **Goal:** free Tamsin from the Penitent Cells beneath the cathedral.
* **Synopsis:**
  * **Lucien Sancroft**, Aurelie's brother, finds her Kindling register: a
    list of 30,000 lower-ward names. He opens the choir doors for the
    company.
  * **Isolde** is in the cathedral, sees the cells, and breaks.
* **Boss: Dame Odile Brask**, Wick-Knight commander of the Church.
* **Choice (Burn #3):** the cells are recall-warded.
  * Burn a memory to break the ward; or
  * let **Isolde** shatter her Vigilglass lens on it (only if her bond was
    kept; her redemption); or
  * free Tamsin alone and leave the other prisoners.
* **Plant:** freed, Tamsin looks at Maren for a long moment: *"We've met.
  Six years ago. You gave me something."* He won't say what yet. He keeps
  confessions sacred.

**Ch 7: "The Quiet Road"**
* **Goal:** parley with Ansel to stop the Long Quiet.
* **Synopsis:**
  * **Juniper**, a blindfolded Quiet assassin, tries to kill Maren and
    fails. Captured, she has no reason to refuse joining.
  * **Ruun** and **Old Kesh** of the Ninth Fire arrive in Candlemere: their
    tellers are losing verses. Kesh reveals that **Ansel is Ruun's brother
    Ans**, and that she told him the story that made him leave.
  * Sefa confesses to Ruun.
  * At the parley at the Hush's edge, Ansel tells Maren the truth of the
    Night of the Long Tide: *you burned your brother, and I followed him
    into the dark.* Maren does not remember Tobin. **Bram does**, faintly.
  * **Kasimir**, who has seen the forgotten children Ansel "shepherds" ask
    for names nobody has, defects. Tobin asked him, *"Do you know my
    name?"*
* **Boss: the Lull.** A drift of sleeping Unremembered that wakes when
  anyone speaks.
* **Choices:**
  * **Vesper's betrayal** (gated by bond rank). Tallis orders him to steal
    Maren's lantern at the parley.
    * Bond high: he refuses, and Tallis burns his collateral, his memory of
      loving Ines.
    * Bond low: he steals the lantern, and Ch 8 opens with getting it back.
  * **Kasimir's first kept memory** (the player chooses which).

**Ch 8: "The Longest Night"**
* **Goal:** stop the Kindling procession before it leaves Candlemere.
* **Synopsis:**
  * The Watch is ordered to herd the lower wards into the procession;
    **Pim's parents**, who sold their memories of Pim, are on the list.
  * Tallis offers **Nox** every memory of her childhood, in exchange for
    the lantern.
  * The battle runs along the Great Bridge against the Church's
    **Reliquary Engine**.
  * **Silas** destroys his still to cut the Church's recall supply.
* **Choice (Burn #4) and side choices:**
  * Burn a memory to break the procession's recall-chains (the gathered
    memories return to their owners). If not, Aurelie leaves with them.
  * Pim: obey or defy.
  * Nox: accept her father's offer or refuse.
* **Ending of Act II:**
  * Aurelie performs a **partial Kindling** at Aurel. A **False Dawn**
    brings a week of real daylight, and the Reach rejoices.
  * Then the Grey Hour shrinks to minutes, and Otto's new estimate is
    **one winter**.
  * In the dark after the False Dawn, Nan hears bells nobody else can:
    *"That's Myrr, dearie. Somebody's still ringing."*

### ACT III: MYRR (What we owe the dead)

**Ch 9: "Into the Hush"**
* **Goal:** cross the Hush to Myrr.
* **Synopsis:**
  * Lantern light fails beyond the Verge, so **Duskborn heroes lead**
    (gameplay inversion).
  * Sound fails too. **Wynn's signing** becomes the company's language,
    and her Telling the only one that works.
  * They walk through forgotten places: a road that unmakes travellers, a
    nursery of nameless toys.
* **Boss: the Forgotten Road** (a survival Night).
* **Plant:** among the toys, Maren finds a tin toy lantern, broken. It feels
  like hers. It isn't.

**Ch 10: "The Bell-City"**
* **Goal:** reach the bell-towers of Myrr.
* **Synopsis:**
  * **Ilka**, translucent and tireless, has rung Myrr's bells every hour
    for 312 years: one bell for each of the Unmourned.
  * The truth comes out: the **Unmourning**, the Lethe engine, and Idony's
    stolen notes.
  * In the archive the company finds **Aldous Wick's** records. The
    Nameless Knight's name is spoken, and he is **restored**. Ines kneels
    to the founder her Order erased.
  * Sefa finds the Ninth Fire's lost verses lying in the Hush like dropped
    beads.
* **Boss: the Unmourned King.** Oswy's shade, commanding his forgotten
  army. Each name the company tells frees a soldier.

**Ch 11: "The Witness"**
* **Goal:** reach the heart of the Hush.
* **Synopsis:**
  * The **Witness** speaks with the voices of the dead: not a god, but
    everyone who ever died, held together by the living's mourning,
    starving.
  * **Tobin's restoration.** If conditions are met (§9), then:
    * Maren's **wick-ash** gives the shape;
    * **Bram's** memory gives the face;
    * **Tamsin** returns Maren's confession;
    * **Ilka** rings a new bell;
    * the company tells his name.

    Tobin walks out of the grey.
  * If the conditions fail, Maren must face **the Boy Without a Name** as a
    boss, and lay him to rest without ever remembering him.
* **Reveal:** Ansel kept one memory unburned: Maren and Tobin laughing on
  the Greywater ferry, the day he took her as an apprentice.

**Ch 12: "The Sun-Altar"**
* **Goal:** return to Aurel before the full Kindling.
* **Synopsis.** The company arrives as Aurelie begins the full Kindling and
  Ansel arrives to extinguish the Altar. Tallis's guards hold the gates; his
  allegiance depends on Nox. Kesh tells Ansel the story of his own
  childhood, the one he burned. Lucien sings. Otto's mirrors and Pell's
  lamp wait for Maren's decision.
* **Final bosses** (by path): **Aurelie, Kindled** (fused with Altar
  light) and/or **the Long Quiet** (Ansel's unmaking tide).
* **Final choice (Burn #5 available).** See §12.

## 10. Burn a memory (narrative mechanic)
**Principle:** burning is the price of the *protective* option. Refusing
always has a visible cost to someone else, so neither path is free.

**Maren's memory pool.** Each burn offers 2–3 options. Bond memories appear
only if that bond is high, which makes the choice hurt more.
| Memory | Removed if burned |
|---|---|
| Her mother's voice singing the ferry song | Maren can no longer hum it. The *Mother's Song* Recollection becomes *Silence*. One Vigil with Bram is lost. |
| The first time Ansel let her carry the lantern | Her Act II confrontation lines with Ansel lose their warmth |

## 17. Writing style guide
Unchanged from v1.
* **Lines:** at most 2 sentences (about 140 characters).
* **Vigils:** 8–20 lines, each with a one-line skip summary.
* **Verbal tics:** Pim apologises mid-arrest; Hester speaks in prices; Nan
  says "dearie"; Kasimir never uses metaphor.
* **Content:** at most one joke per beat of grief. No gore and no sexual
  content. Children are never harmed on screen; Tobin's story is told
  through absence.
* **Names:**
  * The Reach: Anglo-Low Countries in tone.
  * The Ember-folk: short, steppe-flavoured.
  * Myrr: archaic.
