# Hushlight: narrative bible (v1, locked direction)

**Status:** narrative direction locked on 2026-11-23, following approval of
the Hushlight concept. This document is canonical for story, world and
characters. Mechanics are defined in `00_concept_proposal.md`, and later
system documents take precedence on rules.

**What changed since the proposal**
* **Art direction for official artwork:** mostly cartoon, with
  semi-realistic undertones. Bodies, costumes and props stay clean,
  graphic and cel-shaded. The story's emotional register (dark, brooding,
  hopeful) is carried by the faces: expression, eye and brow acting, and
  facial light and shadow. See `02_official_art_style.md`.
* **Maren Vey is hero #1 of the 24.** In battle she is not deployed. She
  stands at the Hearth with her lantern, and her Rites are the player's
  active powers. She still has full official art, a sprite set and a
  character arc.

---

## 1. Narrative direction on one page

**Logline.** In a world where the only light that holds back the dark is
made by burning memories, a Lamplighter who has burned away half her own
past discovers that the burning is what is killing the sun, and must
decide what the world should remember.

**Tone: cartoon surface, serious heart.** The Reach is drawn with warmth
and humour: bickering companions, absurd House politics, a lamp-maker who
names his clockwork. Underneath, the story takes grief, exploitation and
identity seriously, and never resolves them cheaply. The rules:
* **Darkness lives in faces and situations, never in gore.** Unremembered
  fade into drifting motes of light; they do not bleed. Violence is
  stylised.
* **Every comic character has a wound,** and every grim character has one
  thing that makes them laugh.
* **No villain is wrong about everything.** Every antagonist is right
  about at least one cost the heroes would rather not see.
* **Hope is earned through remembering,** never handed out.

**What the story is about** (themes, in priority order):
1. **Memory is identity.** What are you once you have burned what made
   you?
2. **The cost of keeping going.** Who pays for the light?
3. **Grief versus forgetting.** Is forgetting mercy or theft?
4. **Exploitation.** A world that literally burns its poor's past to light
   its rich.
5. **What the living owe the dead.**

**The central dramatic question:** *Should the world keep burning its past
to survive, or carry its past and risk the dark?*

## 2. The world in brief
Cosmology, history and the rules of light are in `00_concept_proposal.md`
§5.1–5.3. They are canonical, summarised here:
* **The Witness** remembered the world into being.
* **The Vigil** (the sun) is the great memory that holds the world in place.
* **Lumen** is the inner light of anything remembered.
* **The Hush** is unmemory.
* **The Unremembered** are forgotten things and people, sliding back into
  the Hush.
* **Recall** is distilled memory that burns as true light.
* **The Dimming** began in year 0 R.D. It is now **312 R.D.**

### 2.1 The Lantern Reach (regions)
| Region | Character | Story role |
|---|---|---|
| **The Greywater Road** | A marsh causeway of ferry towns and waystations, lit by failing beacon-posts | Act I opening; Maren's patrol |
| **Saltreach and the Drowned Coast** | Stilt villages, eel-fishers, a sea that took whole towns during the Long Night | Act I, chapter 2 |
| **The Glass Wastes** | A crystal desert where the Vigil's light fell; scholars' observatories | Act I, chapter 3 |
| **Candlemere** | Lake-city of a thousand lamps: canals, distilleries, House palaces, the Lighthouse Spire | Act I finale; all of Act II |
| **The Ashen Marches** | Steppe of the Ember-folk; firelight, oral law, the Telling | Background; character stories |
| **Aurel** | Mountain sanctum of the Church of the Witness; the Sun-Altar | Act II climax |
| **Myrr, the Unremembered City** | The bell-city everyone forgot, still existing inside the Hush | Act III |

### 2.2 Daily life (so the world exists beyond the battles)
* **Time** is told by the **Grey Hour**, the single dim interval of
  daylight each day. People say "two Greys ago" for "two days ago".
* **The memory trade.** Brokers' stalls post rates on slate boards: *"A
  first kiss: 3 nights' lamp-oil. A childhood summer: 1 week. A dead
  parent's face: 2 weeks."* Sellers often cannot remember afterwards what
  they sold. They simply feel a gap.
* **Last Rites.** When someone dies, the Church harvests their remaining
  memories as tithe. Mourners are forbidden to "hoard the dead".
* **Lantern law.** In Candlemere, being caught unlit after the Grey Hour
  is a crime. The penalty is a memory-fine.
* **Children's rhyme** (foreshadowing the secret): *"Burn a name, lose a
  lane / Burn a song, the road is gone / Burn your mother, and then
  another…"*

## 4. The main story

### Prologue: "The Last Ferry" (tutorial)
At the end of a Grey Hour, Maren escorts a lantern caravan across the
Greywater causeway. A Hush-tide rolls in early. The player learns
placement, light and Rites. Afterwards, Maren feeds her lantern a small
memory (a breakfast), and the player sees the mechanic of burning at no
real cost. Her lantern flickers on a memory she cannot place: a boy's
laugh. **Hook:** the caravan's ledger lists a passenger no one remembers
boarding.

### Act I: "The Greywater Road" (launch campaign, 4 chapters)

**Chapter 1: "The Lamp at Greywater"** (vertical slice)
* **Synopsis.** A Hush-tide is coming. Greywater is a ferry town that sold
  its own history for oil a generation ago, and it is fading. Maren
  arrives to relight the beacon and finds:
  * **Bram**, hiding from House hunters who want his perfect memory;
  * **Sefa**, who refuses to stand inside any lantern-light;
  * **Brother Tamsin**, carrying a Church writ to seize the town's recall
    stores;
  * **Nox**, already halfway through stealing those stores for a House
    client.
  They are forced together by the tide.
* **Boss: the Ferryman.** Greywater's founder, made Unremembered by the
  very burning that saved his town. He poles a ferry of grey passengers
  and asks each hero, "Do you know my name?" Mechanic: he extinguishes the
  lantern nearest him unless a hero standing in light says his name. A
  name restored mid-fight weakens him. This is the first hint of the
  Telling.
* **Choice:**
  * **Burn one of Maren's memories** (burn choice 1 of 5) to relight the
    beacon and save the town; or
  * **Evacuate** and let Greywater go dark.
  Consequences: whether the town survives into Act II, Bram's and Tamsin's
  opinions, and an Archive document.
* **Final frame:** a small hooded figure at the edge of the light, its
  face a smudge of grey. It says one word: *"Sister."*

**Chapter 2: "Candles for the Drowned"**
* The company follows the road to Saltreach. They meet **Wynn**, a deaf
  poacher who hunts Unremembered eels in the dark, and **Nan Corvane**,
  the Hushwife who speaks with the forgotten.
* At a waystation, **Pell** joins: the lamp-maker whose Wicklings bring
  light to where it's needed.
* A distillery village here is visibly losing streets. Doors open onto
  fog. This is the first concrete evidence of the secret, explained away
  by everyone.
* **Boss: the Tidewife.** A drowned town's collective Unremembered, a mass
  of lantern-less windows.
* **Foreshadowing:** Nan tells Maren, "Your lantern's hungry for one
  memory in particular, love. It keeps sniffing at the same door."

**Chapter 3: "The Glass Road"**
* In the Glass Wastes, the observatory scholars **Isolde Grey** and her
  estranged mentor **Otto Venn** have measured the Vigil dimming *faster*
  every decade since recall's invention. They argue over what that means.
* Isolde joins Maren, warm and brilliant. Secretly she sends reports to
  High Witness Aurelie.
* **Boss: the Glass Colossus.** A statue of Saint Idony, forgotten and
  walking.
* **Foreshadowing:** Otto's ledger contains a line in Idony's hand: *"The
  light is borrowed."*

**Chapter 4: "The Lighthouse at Candlemere"**
* **Arrival in the lake-city.** New allies:
  * **Hester Quill**, the disgraced ledger-keeper who knows exactly whose
    memories were bought, and for how little;
  * **Vesper Hale**, a House duelist with a debt;
  * **Constable Pim Arkwright** of the Lamp-Watch;
  * **the Moth**, a vigilante robbing distilleries.
* **The Quiet attacks the Lighthouse Spire,** which burns the memories of
  condemned prisoners. Its leader is unmasked: **Ansel Marrow**, Maren's
  mentor, believed dead. He gave her the lantern she carries.
* **Isolde's betrayal.** She delivers Otto's data and Tamsin's heresy (he
  keeps memories instead of burning them) to Aurelie. Tamsin is arrested.
* **Climax choice:** defend the Spire (keep Candlemere lit; the condemned
  burn), or let the Quiet extinguish it (prisoners spared; the city goes
  dark for a night of riots). **Burn choice 2.**
* **Ending of Act I:** Ansel to Maren: *"What did your light ever cost
  you? You burned it so you wouldn't have to carry it."*

### Act II: "Candlemere" (first expansion), outline
A political thriller in the lake-city.
* **Silas Vash**, a black-market distiller and the last of Saint Idony's
  line, holds her suppressed testament: **recall feeds the Hush**.
* Freeing Tamsin from the Church brings **Lucien Sancroft**, Aurelie's
  brother, to Maren's side.
* **Kasimir** and **Juniper** defect from the Quiet. Each has seen what
  Ansel's peace does to the forgotten.
* **Nox learns she is Corvin Tallis's daughter.** Tallis offers her back
  her stolen memories in exchange for Maren's lantern.
* Aurelie announces the **Great Kindling**: the memories of Candlemere's
  lower wards burned at Aurel's Sun-Altar.
* **Climax:** the Kindling procession. Maren must choose between stopping
  it by force (civil war), exposing the testament (the Church fractures;
  panic), or bargaining with Tallis (the Houses gain control). **Burn
  choices 3 and 4.**

### Act III: "Myrr" (second expansion), outline
The company enters the Hush to reach Myrr, the Unremembered City.
* **Ilka**, the Bell-Warden who has rung Myrr's bells for 312 years,
  guides them.
* **The Nameless Knight's name is restored:** Ser Aldous Wick, first of
  the Lamplighters, whom the Order itself burned out of its histories.
* **Tobin**, if the player has kept enough of Maren's memories and bonds,
  is restored.
* **The revelation:** the Witness is not a god. It is the collected memory
  of everyone who ever died, held together by the living's remembrance.
  It began dying when the living stopped mourning and started burning
  their dead (the Last Rite tithe).
* **Final choice and burn choice 5.**

### Endings
Shaped by the final choice, by how many of Maren's memories remain, and
by key bonds.
| Ending | Requires | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **The Telling** (hopeful, costly) | Bonds with the Ember-folk and Tamsin; Tobin restored | Recall ends. The Reach learns to carry its dead. The Vigil steadies, dimly. Grief returns to the world, and so does meaning. |
| **The Kindling** (dark) | Siding with Aurelie | A century of daylight bought with the lower wards. The game's last frame is a broker's slate with new prices. |
| **The Silence** (bittersweet) | Siding with Ansel | The great lanterns go out. The Unremembered sleep, and so, slowly, does the world. Beautiful and terrible. |
| **The Lantern-Bearer** (hidden) | Maren has burned 4 or more of her 5 offered memories | Maren burns the rest of herself to become a new Witness, remembering everyone. Her companions carry *her* in the Telling. |

## 5. Narrative mechanics
* **Burn a memory (5 choices).** Each choice offers 2–3 of Maren's
  memories, shown as short vignettes. Choosing one removes:
  * the related Vigil scenes;
  * specific dialogue lines, so Maren literally stops referencing it;
  * and changes one Recollection's flavour text.
  The game always warns before the choice and logs it in the Archive as a
  scorched page. **Refusing** (where allowed) has a plot cost instead.
* **Maren's memory pool** (the candidates offered across the game):
  * her mother's voice singing the ferry song;
  * the first time Ansel let her carry the lantern;
  * the smell of the Greywater bakery;
  * the night she nearly quit the Order;
  * Bram teaching her to tie a boatman's knot (only available if their
    bond is high);
  * her first sight of Candlemere's lamps;
  * the last Grey Hour she spent with Tobin, which only becomes visible
    in Act III.
* **Campfire Vigils** happen between Nights. They are the source of bond
  ranks and Recollection unlocks, and they run after defeats too. Every
  scene has Skip, a one-line summary and full rewards.
* **The Telling** in play: restoring names to Unremembered bosses
  mid-fight is a recurring boss mechanic, from the Ferryman onwards.
* **The Archive:** documents, broadsheets and ledger pages. Burned pages
  render with missing passages.

## 6. Antagonists and key non-hero characters
* **Ansel Marrow, the Unlit Shepherd** (60s). Maren's mentor. Once the
  Order's most celebrated Lamplighter, he burned nearly all of himself on
  the roads and discovered peace in the emptiness. Soft-spoken, kind,
  immovable. He does not hate light; he pities it. He keeps one memory
  unburned: the day he met Maren. He will not tell her why.
* **High Witness Aurelie Sancroft** (40s). Architect of the Great
  Kindling. She has never burned a memory, because she believes she owes
  the Vigil a full heart at death. The Hush took her parish when she was
  nine, and she still sleeps with three lamps lit. Her arithmetic is
  sincere: one ward against a century.
* **Magnate Corvin Tallis** (50s). First among the Lantern Houses. Elegant,
  dry, generous to his staff, ruthless to everyone else. His daughter
  Noemi sold every memory of him to escape him. He funds the Church and
  the Quiet both, and would trade the world to be recognised by her once.
* **The Ferryman** (Chapter 1 boss). Greywater's founder, Unremembered. He
  wants only his name.
* **Saint Idony Vash** (historical). Discoverer of recall. Her final
  testament, suppressed by the Church, reads: *"The light is borrowed.
  Every flame we strike is paid for by the world's own remembering. God
  forgive me, I have taught them to eat the house to warm the hearth."*
* **Lantern-Marshal Corwen Ashby** (50s). Head of the Order. A decent
  bureaucrat presiding over decline, who knows the Order burned its
  founder's name and has never told anyone.

### 7.2 Hero entries

Each entry gives the art brief (look and expression range), the character

#### 1. Maren Vey: Lamplighter of the Greywater Road
*Commander · Order · 24 · she/her*
* **Look.**
  * Lean and weathered, with short cropped dark-auburn hair she cuts
    herself.
  * A long oilskin Lamplighter coat in road-blue, its brass buttons
    mismatched (replaced on the road).
  * Fingerless gloves; a scarf of faded ferry-town knitwork.
  * Her **Old Lantern** hangs from a hooked pole taller than she is. It is
    brass and soot-black, with a warm amber flame.
  * One thin burn-scar on her left jaw.
  * **Expression range:** steady, guarded resolve by default. Rare, sudden
    warmth. Hollow stillness when she burns a memory.
* **Who she is.** Dry, practical, quietly funny. She does the job no one
  thanks her for and pretends not to mind. She is kind in actions and
  clumsy in words.
* **Want:** keep her road lit, and prove her mentor's lantern wasn't
  wasted on her.
* **Fear:** becoming "a lantern with no one inside".
* **Contradiction:** she preaches *burn only what is yours* and has burned
  everything she loved, including her brother.
* **Arc:** from a dutiful burner to someone who chooses what to carry. The
  player decides how much of her survives.
* **Combat.** Stands at the Hearth. Rites: *Flare*, *Beacon* and *Last
  Ember*. Recollections reshape her Rites; for example, *Mother's Song*
  makes Flare also heal allies in light.
* **Bonds:** Tobin (brother); Bram (too little memory and too much);
  Ines (rival); Ansel (mentor and antagonist; story-only).
* **Voice:** *"Stay in the light. If you can't, stay near me."*

#### 2. Bram Ostrow: The Man Who Cannot Forget
*Vanguard · L · former House distillery guard · 44 · he/him*
* **Look.**
  * A mountain of a man, slope-shouldered, with a gentle round face, a
    close beard and a balding crown.
  * A patched guard's coat with the House Tallis badge torn off.
  * His **tower shield** is a riveted distillery vat door with the gauge
    still on it.
  * **Expression range:** soft sorrow; startled embarrassment; terrifying
    calm when protecting someone.
* **Who he is.** Hyperthymesic: he remembers every day of his life in full.
  His mind is worth a fortune, so the Houses want to drain him. He is
  gentle, slow to anger, and carries every grief he has ever had at full
  weight.
* **Want:** to be left alone; then, to be useful to someone who doesn't
  see him as a vault.
* **Fear:** being tapped like a cask.
* **Contradiction:** he envies people who can forget, and despises them
  for doing it.
* **Arc:** learns the Telling, giving memories away by speaking them
  rather than hoarding or burning them. He becomes the company's living
  archive.
* **Combat.** *Weight of Years*: damage reduction grows the longer he
  holds his tile. *Vat-Door Bash* shoves the front enemy back one tile,
  into the dark if possible. Recollections choose between becoming an
  immovable wall and a shove-chain controller.
* **Bonds:** Maren; Hester (she knows what he is worth and never tries to
  sell it).
* **Voice:** *"I remember you. That's not a threat. It's a promise."*

#### 3. Sefa of the Ninth Fire: The Exile Who Sold the Telling
*Marksman · E · Ember-folk (exiled) · 26 · she/her*
* **Look.**
  * Lithe and sun-dark, with a long braid threaded with ember-glass beads
    that glow faintly.
  * Ember-folk tattoos of flame lines on her forearms.
  * A layered steppe coat in rust and ochre.
  * A recurve bow of horn and blackwood; fire arrows in a quiver over her
    right shoulder.
  * **Expression range:** proud sneer; fierce focus; a guilty flinch when
    the Telling is mentioned.
* **Who she is.** Sharp-tongued and brave to the point of spite. She
  despises lanterns as theft dressed as salvation and stands at the edge
  of every light on principle.
* **Want:** to win back her place at the Ninth Fire.
* **Fear:** that she deserved the exile.
* **Contradiction:** the woman who scorns the memory trade once sold her
  clan's sacred Telling to a House, to buy her sister's cure.
* **Arc:** confesses, faces Ruun, and teaches the Telling to outsiders. She
  becomes what her clan feared and what the world needs.
* **Combat.** Fire arrows **briefly reveal** veiled enemies, so others can
  hit them. She deals more damage while standing in dim light.
* **Bonds:** Ruun (her chief); Old Kesh (her teacher); Pim (an arresting
  officer turned friend; comic); Wynn (rival archers).
* **Voice:** *"Your lamps steal. My fire only burns."*

#### 4. Brother Tamsin Reed: The Confessor Who Keeps
*Warden · L · Church of the Witness · 35 · he/him*
* **Look.**
  * Tall and thin, with a soft-featured face and very tired eyes behind
    round spectacles; close-cropped greying hair.
  * A bell-shaped confessor's robe in cream and soot-grey, with a deep
    hood.
  * His staff holds a hanging **censer-lantern** that breathes pale smoke.
  * **Expression range:** gentle attentiveness; exhausted kindness; rare,
    luminous anger.
* **Who he is.** A Church confessor who "takes your worst memory so you
  can sleep". In secret he never burns them. He keeps them, and he is full
  of strangers' pain.
* **Want:** to ease suffering without adding to it.
* **Fear:** that his faith is a lie he keeps because he cannot afford to
  lose it.
* **Contradiction:** he is a priest of a Church whose central sacrament he
  secretly refuses.
* **Arc:** is arrested for heresy (Act I) and freed (Act II). He chooses
  between the Church he loves and the truth he keeps. His Recollection can
  give the kept memories back to their owners.
* **Combat.** *Mending*: heals by taking a share of an ally's damage onto
  himself, then converting it to a shield. Strongest in bright tiles.
* **Bonds:** Lucien (brother in faith); Nan Corvane (faith against
  witchcraft).
* **Voice:** *"Give it to me. I'll carry it awhile."*

#### 5. Nox (Noemi Castell): The Thief Without a Childhood
*Stalker · D · Candlemere underworld · 22 · she/her*
* **Look.**
  * Small and wiry, with a sharp fox-like face, dark bobbed hair and a
    shaved undercut.
  * A long charcoal scarf she never removes; a hooded leather jacket with
    many pockets; two hooked daggers.
  * **Expression range:** smirking bravado; cold professional blankness; a
    lost child's look when she touches a memory vial.
* **Who she is.** A memory thief who sold her own childhood years ago and
  doesn't know why. Funny, mercenary, fiercely loyal once won.
* **Want:** enough money to never belong to anyone.
* **Fear:** the gap in herself where her past should be.
* **Contradiction:** she steals the very thing she gave away.
* **Arc:** discovers she is Corvin Tallis's daughter (Act II), and that she
  sold her memories to escape him. She chooses whether to take them back.
* **Combat.** *Stealth* in dark tiles: untargetable until she strikes.
  *Snuff Strike* executes veiled enemies below a health threshold.
* **Bonds:** Vesper (both owe Tallis); Cass (rival thieves).
* **Voice:** *"Everybody's got something worth stealing. Mostly it's
  their past."*

#### 6. Pellam "Old Pell" Fosse: The Lamp-Maker
*Tinker · L · independent · 71 · he/him*
* **Look.** Short and hunched, with a magnificent white moustache and
  goggles pushed up on a bald head. A leather apron. A **backpack rack of
  three small lanterns** that clink. Clockwork *Wicklings* (knee-high
  lantern automata) waddle after him. **Expression range:** grumpy
  squint; delighted tinkerer's grin; a private, devastated tenderness when
  touching his locket.
* **Who he is.** He builds lamps and complains about everything. He keeps
  his late wife's memory unburned in a locket, and refuses to burn it even
  to save his own life.

## 9. Production priority: which heroes come first, and why

### Wave 1 (now): **Maren, Bram, Sefa, Tamsin, Nox**
1. **They are the vertical slice.** Milestone 3 needs at least four
   distinctive playable heroes and one complete chapter. Chapter 1's story
   is built on exactly these five: Maren at the Hearth, with Bram, Sefa,
   Tamsin and Nox deployed.
2. **They exercise every core rule.** Their roles are tank, ranged
   damage, healer and assassin, the four that define any autobattler
   squad. Their affinities span Lightsworn (Bram, Tamsin), Edgewalker
   (Sefa) and Duskborn (Nox), so every light rule (mana in light,
   dim-edge bonuses, stealth in dark, revealing veiled enemies) has a hero
   who tests it.
3. **They set the visual language for every faction.** Their costumes
   anchor the Order (Maren), House labour (Bram), the Ember-folk (Sefa),
   the Church (Tamsin) and the Candlemere underworld (Nox). The other 19
   heroes reuse these palettes and costume rules, so getting these five
   right first prevents redrawing later.
4. **They carry Act I's emotional spine.** Maren and Bram (memory too
   little against too much), Tamsin's secret, Sefa's guilt and Nox's
   missing past are the threads every later chapter pays off.
5. **Maren is the face of the game.** Key art, store page, every dialogue
   scene, and the on-board Hearth.

### Wave 2 (next): **Tobin, Isolde, Pell, Ines, the Nameless Knight**
* **Tobin:** the central mystery. Chapter 1's final frame needs at least
  his hooded design.
* **Isolde:** the Act I betrayal must be seeded from Chapter 3.
* **Pell:** the Tinker role and mobile light sources, the third pillar of
  the light system.
* **Ines:** the Order rival and a moving light source.
* **The Nameless Knight:** proof that the enemy are people, with a cameo
  in Chapter 2.

## 10. Writing style guide (for every scene)
* **Mobile constraints.** Lines of at most 2 sentences (about 140
  characters). Vigil scenes of 8–20 lines. Every scene has a one-line
  summary for skips.
* **Voice.** Each hero has a verbal tic list. For example: Pim apologises
  mid-arrest; Hester speaks in prices; Nan calls everyone "dearie"; Kasimir
  never uses metaphors.
* **Show the cost.** Whenever light is made, someone pays, on screen or in
  a line.
* **Humour rule.** At most one joke per beat of grief, and never at the
  grief's expense.
* **Content boundaries.** No gore; no sexual content; death is shown
  through fading and motes. Child characters are never harmed on screen.
  Tobin's story is told through absence, not violence.
* **Names and language.** Reach names are Anglo-Low Countries in tone
  (Ostrow, Calder, Quill). Ember-folk names are short and steppe-flavoured
  (Sefa, Ruun, Kesh). Myrr names are archaic (Ilka). Avoid real-world
  slurs and religious terms beyond generic ones ("Witness", "Vigil",
  "Rite").
