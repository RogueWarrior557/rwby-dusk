## 1. The story in one page

**The world.** The sun, called **the Vigil**, is dying. The only light that
holds back the dark (**the Hush**) is made by distilling memories into oil
and burning them. Cities glow with lamps fuelled by childhoods sold at
market. The poor sell their past to light the rich. The Church harvests the
memories of the dead. People and places that no one remembers anymore
become **the Unremembered**: grey, hungry shapes that want only their
names.

**The magic.** All power is **lumen**, the light of remembrance. People draw
it from five sources:
* their own memories;
* fallen sunlight crystals (**Vigilglass**);
* nine living sun-coals guarded by the Ember-folk (**Sunseed**);
* being remembered by others (**Renown**);
* the dark itself (**Hushcraft**), which slowly makes the world forget you.

Seven Arts shape lumen: burning, oath-holding, healing by telling, lens
focusing, story-fire, shadow, and artifice. An eighth, **Naming**, is
legendary and lost.

**The heroine.** **Maren Vey, the Greywater Flame**, is the Order of
Lamplighters' youngest Captain in ninety years and a living legend:
* She is **Wick-born**, a once-a-century gift. One memory of hers can light
  a city.
* She carries the **First Lantern**, a founder's relic and the only key to
  the Church's Sun-Altar.
* She fights at the front with its hooked pole as a glaive.
* At 18 she held a causeway **alone, all night**, against the dark, and saved
  300 people.

She doesn't know what that light cost: she burned her little brother
**Tobin** out of existence to make it.

**The secret.** Burning memory doesn't just empty a mind; it unravels the
world. Three hundred years ago, a king of the bell-city of **Myrr** forced
everyone to forget his war-dead. That wound started the Dimming. Saint
Idony's "miracle" of recall was copied from that king's machine. **Every
lamp is a small Unmourning.** The cure has been the disease all along.

**The conflict.** Three powers race toward one altar:
* the **Church**, which plans to burn the memories of 30,000 poor citizens
  to buy a century of daylight;
* **the Quiet**, led by Maren's lost mentor **Ansel**, who wants to put out
  every great light and let the world forget in peace;
* the **Lantern Houses**, which fund both sides and want to sell the sun
  itself.

**The answer Maren finds** is the hardest: **remember instead of burn.**
Telling the names of the dead aloud makes faint light and costs nothing but
grief. It could save the world, and it could bring Tobin back, if enough of
Maren is left to remember him.

**The player's role.** You command the battles. At five moments, you choose
whether Maren **burns one of her own memories** to protect others. Every
burn removes real scenes from the story. Four endings follow: hopeful,
dark, bittersweet and one hidden.

## 2. The story, chapter by chapter

**Prologue: The Last Ferry.** Maren guards a caravan across the marsh road.
A ledger lists "Lamplighter Vey and one minor" from six years ago. She has
always travelled alone.

### Act I: The Greywater Road (*what light costs*)
1. **The Lamp at Greywater.** A fading ferry town; four strangers forced
   together by a dark tide: Bram, Sefa, Tamsin and Nox. The boss is the
   town's forgotten founder, who only wants his name. Maren either burns a
   memory to save the town or evacuates it. Final frame: a faceless boy
   whispers *"Sister."*
2. **Candles for the Drowned.** On the coast, Wynn's drowned village rises
   as a single grieving creature. Free its people by naming them, or seal
   them away. A mysterious knight appears and vanishes.
3. **The Glass Road.** Scientists prove the sun has **seven winters** left.
   Isolde, warm and brilliant, joins, and is secretly reporting to the
   Church.
4. **The Lighthouse at Candlemere.** The great lamp-city. The Church
   announces the **Great Kindling**. The Quiet attacks the Lighthouse, and
   its leader is **Ansel**, Maren's mentor, alive. Isolde betrays the
   company and Tamsin is arrested. Ansel: *"What did your light ever cost
   you?"* The faceless boy takes Ansel's hand.

### Act II: Candlemere (*who pays*)
5. **The Undercity Still.** Hunted, the company robs House Tallis's memory
   vault and finds Saint Idony's suppressed confession: *"The light is
   borrowed."* The mysterious knight joins.
6. **The Choir and the Cell.** They free Tamsin from the Church's cells,
   helped by the High Witness's own brother. Isolde gets her chance at
   redemption.
7. **The Quiet Road.** At a parley in the dark, Ansel tells Maren the truth:
   *you burned your brother.* She can't remember him. Bram can, faintly.
   Ansel's own guardian defects. Vesper faces a betrayal order.
8. **The Longest Night.** The Kindling procession on the Great Bridge. The
   High Witness makes Maren an offer: *give yourself to the Altar and I'll
   spare all 30,000.* Pim must choose between the law and their parents;
   Nox, between her father's offer and her friends. A partial Kindling causes a **False Dawn**: a week
   of sunlight, then a faster fall. **One winter left.** In the dark, bells
   ring from a city no one remembers.

### Act III: Myrr (*what we owe the dead*)
9. **Into the Hush.** Lanterns fail; the shadow-dwellers lead. Sound fails,
   and Wynn's sign language becomes the only Telling that works.
10. **The Bell-City.** Myrr, forgotten for 312 years, where one woman has
    rung a bell for every forgotten soldier. The truth of the Unmourning
    comes out. The knight's name is restored: **Aldous Wick**, founder of the
    Order, erased by his own brothers. Maren learns her lantern holds the
    lens of the machine that destroyed Myrr, and **awakens Naming**: the
    power to restore the forgotten and make light without burning.
11. **The Witness.** The "god" is everyone who has ever died, starving
    because the living burn instead of mourning. If enough of Maren
    remains, the company restores **Tobin**. If not, she must lay to rest a
    brother she never remembers.
12. **The Sun-Altar.** The Kindling and the Silence collide. Maren decides
    the world's answer, and perhaps burns the last thing she has: her own
    name.

## 3. The 24 heroes' own stories

Each entry: **before the story · during the story · key scene · where it
can end.**

---

### 1. Maren Vey: *the Greywater Flame*
*Lamplighter-Captain · Order of Lamplighters · 24 · Wick-born · bearer of the First Lantern*
* **Before.** Born in Greywater. Her mother drowned in a dark tide when
  Maren was nine; her father sold his memories until he forgot his
  children. Maren raised her brother Tobin alone. At 16, the legendary
  Lamplighter Ansel Marrow took her as his apprentice, because he could see
  what she was: **Wick-born**, a once-a-century soul whose memories burn like
  bonfires. On the Night of the Long Tide, Ansel, nearly empty, gave her the
  **First Lantern**, the founders' relic, which only a Wick-born can hold.
  To keep it lit she burned her last memories of Tobin. That single burning
  blazed so bright she **held the Greywater causeway alone, all night**, and
  saved more than 300 travellers. The Order made her its **youngest Captain
  in ninety years**, and ferry towns sing her ballad. Nobody remembers the
  boy. She gave the memory of *choosing* to a confessor so she could sleep.
* **Who she is.** Bold, wry, commanding: the first into the dark, every
  time. Proud of a legend she doesn't understand. She treats burning as her
  right ("they're *my* memories") and keeps everyone at arm's length because
  she has no past to share.
* **Her power.**
  * She fights at the front with the **lantern-glaive**, sweeping arcs of
    burning light. Her lantern is a moving light that the whole squad
    orbits.
  * Her ultimate, **Last Ember**, burns a memory for a battlefield-clearing
    detonation.
  * She grows down one of two paths, shaped by the player's story choices:
    the **Ember Path** (burn, and become devastating) or the **Remembrance
    Path** (keep, and strengthen everyone around her).
  * In Act III she becomes the **first Namer in 312 years**: she can restore
    the forgotten and make light without fuel.
* **During.**
  * **Act I:** every faction starts circling her lantern, the only key to
    the Church's Sun-Altar.
  * **Act II:** her legend unravels. Her burning created the Quiet, and the
    High Witness offers to spare 30,000 people if Maren burns *herself* on
    the Altar instead.
  * **Act III:** she learns her lantern is built from the lens of the
    machine that erased Myrr. The power that destroyed a city is inside her,
    and she must decide whether anyone should hold it.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 11. Holding Bram's memory of a face, Tamsin's kept
  confession and six years of ash, Maren speaks a name she has never known,
  *Tobin*, and her brother walks out of the grey.
* **Endings.** She restores her brother and teaches the Reach to grieve
  (Long Telling). She sits beside Ansel as the lights go out (Silence).
  She becomes the city's hero and hates herself (Kindling). She burns
  herself on the Altar to spare 30,000 (the Offering). Or she burns her own
  name to become the world's memory (Lantern-Bearer).

### 2. Bram Ostrow: *the man who cannot forget*
*Vanguard · former House guard · 44*
* **Before.** Born with perfect memory: every day of his life, in full. He
  guarded the House Tallis distillery in Candlemere and saw the 309 vat
  rupture, when burned memories came alive as a creature called **the
  Residue**. Tallis covered it up and planned to "tap" Bram's priceless
  mind. He fled. Years before that, working the Greywater ferry, he saw a
  young Lamplighter apprentice travelling with a boy who carried a tin toy
  lantern.
* **During.** In Act I he hides at Greywater and joins Maren reluctantly.
  He tells her, *"You came through here with a boy,"* and she insists she
  never had one. In Act II his memory is the eyewitness proof of the
  Residue, and the only living memory of Tobin's face, though the name
  keeps slipping away. He learns the Telling: giving memories by *speaking*
  them, not hoarding them.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 11. He describes Tobin's gap-toothed grin while
  the company tells the name, and Tobin's face comes back into focus.
* **Endings.** He becomes the Reach's living archive, the first Teller of
  the new age. In the Lantern-Bearer ending, Bram carries Maren's story.

### 3. Sefa of the Ninth Fire: *the exile who sold her people's song*
*Marksman · Ember-folk (exiled) · 26*
* **Before.** Her younger sister Taya was dying of grey-lung, and the only
  cure cost more than her clan had. A House broker offered a fortune for
  the Ninth Fire's **Telling**, the clan's sacred spoken history. Sefa
  recited it into a broker's bottle as memory, and Taya lived. The bottle
  was burned in some rich man's lamp, and the clan's tellers began
  forgetting verses. Chief Ruun exiled her. On her way out she stole a
  **spark of the Ninth Fire's Sunseed** and hid it in her braid beads. It
  powers her **Storyfire** arrows, and it is a second crime she has told
  no one about.
* **During.** In Act I she refuses to stand in lantern-light and mocks the
  memory trade, from guilt. In Act II Ruun and the elder Kesh arrive in
  Candlemere because the tellers keep losing verses, and Sefa confesses
  before the whole company. In Act III she finds the lost verses lying in
  the Hush like dropped beads, because forgotten things go there.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 10. She kneels in the grey and gathers the verses
  one by one, speaking each aloud so they can't be lost again.
* **Endings.** She returns to the Ninth Fire and opens the Telling to
  outsiders, the thing her clan feared most and the world needs most. If
  Ruun refuses to forgive her, she teaches it in Candlemere instead.

### 4. Brother Tamsin Reed: *the confessor who keeps what he's given*
*Warden · Church of the Witness · 35*
* **Before.** A travelling confessor whose sacrament is to take a
  penitent's worst memory "so they can sleep", then burn it in the Church's
  lamps. In secret he never burns them. He keeps them all and lives with
  strangers' grief. Six years ago, in Greywater, a shaking 18-year-old
  Lamplighter gave him the memory of *a choice she made on the causeway*.
  He has carried it ever since.
* **During.** In Act I he arrives with a writ to seize Greywater's recall
  stores and half-recognises Maren. In Chapter 4 Isolde exposes his
  heresy and he is arrested. Freed in Chapter 6, he tells Maren, *"You gave
  me something,"* but keeps the seal of confession until she is ready.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 11. He returns Maren's confession: she remembers
  choosing to burn *someone she loved*. That completes the shape of Tobin.
* **Endings.** He becomes the first priest of the Witness as it truly is,
  the dead, and returns every kept memory to its owner. In the Kindling
  ending he is burned as a heretic. Choosing this ending means accepting
  that.

### 5. Nox (Noemi Castell): *the thief without a childhood*
*Stalker · Candlemere underworld · 22*
* **Before.** Born **Noemi Tallis**, the Magnate's only child. At 14 she sold
  every memory of her father to a broker and vanished, taking the name
  "Castell" from the broker's ledger. She doesn't remember why. She only
  knows there's a hole in her, and that she steals memories for a living.
* **During.** In Act I she is caught robbing Greywater's stores and stays
  because the money's better. In Chapter 5's heist she reads Hester's
  ledger: *"N. Tallis, aged 14, sold as Castell."* In Chapter 8 her father
  offers her childhood back in exchange for Maren's lantern.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 8, on the bridge. She holds the vial of her own
  childhood, and the player decides whether she drinks it, becoming who she
  was, or walks away, choosing who she is.
* **Endings.** If she refuses, Tallis (to win her respect) turns on the
  Church at the Sun-Altar, but only if she has true friends in Vesper or
  Cass. If she accepts, she gains a father and loses the woman her friends
  knew.

### 6. Pellam "Old Pell" Fosse: *the lamp-maker who hates what lamps eat*
*Tinker · independent · 71*
* **Before.** Built lamps for fifty years. His wife Marta died in 299. He
  keeps one memory of her unburned in a locket: their wedding dance on a
  frozen canal. He has refused, repeatedly, to burn it even to save his own
  life.
* **During.** In Act I he joins at a coast waystation with his clockwork
  Wicklings, which he names after cheeses. In Act II he quietly tries to
  build a **lamp that needs no recall** and fails, over and over. In Act III
  he learns why: it doesn't need fuel, it needs listeners.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 12. Pell tells the company about Marta's dance,
  and his empty lamp flickers to life. It is the first light ever made
  without burning.
* **Endings.** "Pell lamps" light the Reach in the Long Telling ending. In
  the others, he keeps his locket and dies, stubborn and loved.

### 7. Wynn Sallow: *the silent hunter of the drowned*
*Marksman · Drowned Coast · 33*
* **Before.** Deaf since birth. Her village, **Lowmere**, drowned in a dark
  tide when she was 17, and everyone she knew became Unremembered under the
  sea. Raised by the Hushwife Nan, she hunts Unremembered eels in the dark
  for the Houses' "deep-recall".
* **During.** In Act I her village rises as the **Tidewife**, the Chapter 2
  boss, with her mother inside it. She can free them by signing their
  names. In Act III sound fails in the Hush, so her sign language becomes
  the company's only voice and the only Telling that works there.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 9. In total silence, Wynn signs Nan's true name,
  and the fading Hushwife sharpens back into colour.
* **Endings.** She leads a "silent Telling" movement for the deaf and the
  voiceless. If the Tidewife was sealed, she must fight her own mother's
  shadow in Act III.

### 8. Nan Corvane, the Hushwife: *the woman nobody remembers*
*Arcanist · independent · 76*
* **Before.** Decades ago she traded her own name to the Hush to learn to
  speak with the forgotten. Since then almost no one can hold onto who she
  is, except Wynn, the girl she raised.
* **During.** In Act I she guides the coast and warns Maren that her
  lantern's ash "wants out". If the Tidewife is freed, she admits she is
  fading. In Act II she hears the bells of Myrr in the dark. In Act III she
  guides the company through the Hush, the place she half-belongs to.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 9. She chooses to be remembered, and lets Wynn
  speak (sign) her true name: **Annick**.
* **Endings.** Restored, she becomes the Reach's first "name-keeper" for
  the Unremembered. Unrestored, she walks into the Hush with a smile and
  stays a story.

### 9. Isolde Grey: *the scientist who chose the sacrifice*
*Arcanist · Glass Wastes · 29*
* **Before.** An orphan of the Glass Wastes, raised by the engineer Otto
  Venn like a daughter. When Otto's warning was laughed out of the College,
  High Witness Aurelie offered Isolde funding and a way to *act*: the
  Kindling. Isolde did the arithmetic (one ward against a century) and
  said yes.
* **During.** In Act I she joins in Chapter 3. Coded letters to Aurelie hide
  in the Archive. In Chapter 4 she betrays the company, handing over Otto's
  data and Tamsin's heresy. In Act II, in the cathedral, she sees the cells
  and the 30,000 names and breaks.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 6. If the player kept faith with her, she smashes
  her irreplaceable Vigilglass lens against the cell ward to free the
  prisoners.
* **Endings.** Redeemed, she focuses the Telling-light at the Sun-Altar and
  reconciles with Otto. Unredeemed, she stands beside Aurelie, and in the
  Kindling ending she is the one who throws the switch.

### 10. Ottoline "Otto" Venn: *the woman who was right too early*
*Tinker · Glass Wastes · 64*
* **Before.** She measured the Dimming's acceleration and proved it began in
  163 R.D., the year the Last Rite tithe began. The College rejected her
  paper in 309 and she retreated to her desert observatory with a brass leg
  brace and a grudge.
* **During.** In Act I she gives the clock: seven winters. In Act II
  Isolde's betrayal is the second time someone she loved chose the
  institution over her. In Act III her mirrors become the way the
  Telling-light travels across Aurel.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 12. She angles her great dish-mirror and says to
  Isolde, without looking at her, *"Hold the other side. I can't reach."*
* **Endings.** Vindicated, she becomes head of a new College. Or, in the
  Silence ending, she records the last sunset for no one.

### 11. Hester Quill: *the ledger that wouldn't lie*
*Warden · former House ledger-keeper · 52*
* **Before.** Twenty years keeping House Tallis's books. She knew exactly
  whose memories were bought and for how little, and told herself it was
  arithmetic. In 310 she refused to falsify the ledger hiding the House's
  purchases of **children's** memories. She was disgraced, and kept a copy
  in her head.
* **During.** In Act I she joins in Candlemere and stares at the name
  "Castell". In Act II her stolen ledgers (recovered in Chapter 5) prove the
  child purchases and reveal Nox's identity. She becomes the conscience of
  the company, and its most frightening negotiator.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 5. She tells Nox the truth from the ledger, gently,
  in prices, because that's the only way she knows how to say it.
* **Endings.** Her ledgers bring down the memory market (Long Telling). In
  the Kindling ending she keeps the books for the new order and despises
  herself.

### 12. Vesper Hale: *the duelist who pawned his heart*
*Blade · sworn to House Tallis · 28*
* **Before.** Grew up in Candlemere's lantern-ward beside **Ines Calder**,
  his first and only love, though he never told her before she left for the
  Order. To pay his mother's memory-fines he borrowed from House Tallis.
  The collateral is his memory of loving Ines. Fail a contract and Tallis
  burns it.
* **During.** In Act I he joins in Candlemere, flirting with everyone except
  Ines, who thinks him a shallow peacock. In Chapter 7 Tallis orders him to
  steal Maren's lantern at the parley.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 7. If his bond with the company is high, he
  refuses, and Tallis burns the memory. He doesn't know why he suddenly
  feels lighter and sadder. In Chapter 10 Ines finds out what he gave up.
* **Endings.** Ines tells him the story of his own love (a Telling). He
  doesn't remember it, but he believes her, and that's enough. If he
  betrayed the company, he can return only if Nox vouches for him.

### 13. Pim Arkwright: *the constable looking for a family*
*Marksman · Candlemere Lamp-Watch · 20*
* **Before.** When Pim was 12, their parents sold every memory of their
  child to pay the rent. They still live two streets away and nod politely
  when Pim walks past. Pim joined the Lamp-Watch to belong to something
  that would see them.
* **During.** In Act I they arrest half the party at least once, earnestly.
  In Act II the Watch shows what it's for: protecting the Houses. In
  Chapter 8 Pim is ordered to herd the lower wards, including their
  parents, into the Kindling procession.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 8. Pim tears off their badge and fires a flare that
  lights the whole bridge, so the crowd can see who's herding them.
* **Endings.** Pim founds a new Watch that protects people. If they obeyed,
  they're promoted and can never look at their parents again.

### 14. Cass Allard, "the Moth": *the hero who couldn't give it back*
*Stalker · vigilante · 34*
* **Before.** A distillery stoker who shovelled bottled childhoods into the
  furnaces. He was there with Bram the night of the vat rupture. His wife
  **Mira** sold her memories of their marriage to pay their debts and no
  longer knows him. He became the Moth to rob memory vaults and return what
  was stolen. His secret: to fight, he **drinks** stolen recall
  (*Draughting*, a forbidden art). It gives him bursts of strength and
  leaves him living strangers' memories for hours.
* **During.** In Act I he is Candlemere's folk hero, chased by Pim. In Act II
  he finally steals back Mira's memories in the Chapter 5 heist. She
  drinks them, and their marriage feels to her like a stranger's story,
  because what was burned had already faded from the world. It is the
  cruellest proof of the secret.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 5, afterwards. Mira, kind and puzzled, says, *"I
  believe you. I'm sorry I can't feel it."*
* **Endings.** He learns the only way back is new memories, made slowly. He
  and Mira start over in the Long Telling epilogue. In the Silence ending,
  neither remembers.

### 15. Ines Calder: *the shield of an Order that lied*
*Vanguard · Order of Lamplighters · 31*
* **Before.** The Order's poster knight, devout and flawless, who believed
  Ansel's lantern should have gone to her. She left her childhood ward, and
  a boy named Vesper, to serve.
* **During.** In Act I she arrives in Chapter 3 to summon Maren to an
  inquiry, and joins under orders in Chapter 4. In Act II the Order stays
  neutral while the Church cages heretics, and her faith cracks. In Act
  III, in Myrr, she learns the Order burned its own founder, Aldous Wick,
  for telling the truth.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 10. She kneels to Wick, then stands and tears the
  Order's sigil from her shield.
* **Endings.** She rebuilds the Order around Wick's original oath (Long
  Telling). In the Kindling ending she becomes Lantern-Marshal of a Church
  militia.

### 16. The Nameless Knight (Ser Aldous Wick): *the founder they erased*
*Blade · Unremembered · 312 years forgotten*
* **Before.** The eighth founder of the Order of Lamplighters. In 48 R.D. he
  found Myrr's records and warned that recall was a small Unmourning. The
  Order, afraid of panic, burned his name in the Wick Purge, and history
  remembers "Seven Apprentices". He has wandered the roads ever since,
  defending travellers who can't remember him.
* **During.** In Act I he holds a causeway in Chapter 2 and vanishes. In Act
  II he is found sleeping in a forgotten Order chapel in Chapter 5, beside a
  statue with its name chiselled off, and joins. In Act III his name is
  spoken in Myrr and he is restored.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 10. His blurred face sharpens. His first words in
  312 years with a name: *"Ah. That's who I was. I'd hoped for someone
  braver."*
* **Endings.** He finally rests, or stays to rebuild the Order beside Ines.
  In the Silence ending he fades again, contentedly.

### 17. Silas Vash: *the bill for a miracle*
*Tinker · black-market distiller · 46*
* **Before.** The last descendant of Saint Idony. His family was quietly
  given the Church's sealed copy of her testament as a "relic" no one was
  allowed to read. He read it at 15. He runs an illegal still in the
  undercity, out of bitterness and because he's good at it.
* **During.** In Act II he guides the vault heist and reads the testament
  aloud to the company. In Chapter 8 he destroys his own still to cut the
  Church's recall supply.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 8. He lights the fuse on his life's work: *"Great-
  great-grandmother saved the world. I'm the bill. Consider it paid."*
* **Endings.** He becomes the man who dismantles the distilleries (Long
  Telling). In the Kindling ending he is the Church's master distiller,
  coerced, with a fume-mask he never takes off.

### 18. Lucien Sancroft: *the choir-mage and his sister*
*Arcanist · Church choir · 25*
* **Before.** Raised by his older sister Aurelie after their parish was
  swallowed by the Hush. He worships her almost as much as the Witness.
  His hymns can bend light.
* **During.** In Chapter 6 he finds her Kindling register, 30,000
  lower-ward names in her handwriting, and opens the choir doors for the
  company. In Act III he must face her at the Sun-Altar.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 12. He sings the old parish lullaby she sang to
  him, and Aurelie falters for the first time in the game.
* **Endings.** He brings his sister down, or brings her back (Long
  Telling). In the Kindling ending he leads the hymn of a century of
  daylight and never sings again afterwards.

### 19. Kasimir Lowe, "the Unlit Wall": *the man who remembers nothing*
*Vanguard · the Quiet (defector) · 38*
* **Before.** A former Watch officer who burned himself empty after his
  family died, and was found by Ansel. He believes in the Quiet because he
  remembers no reason not to.
* **During.** In Act I he is the Chapter 4 boss, guarding the Lighthouse
  gate. In Act II he watches Ansel "shepherd" forgotten children in the
  Hush. A grey-faced boy asks him, *"Do you know my name?"* He doesn't,
  and that is the first thing that has ever hurt him. He defects in
  Chapter 7.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 7. The player chooses Kasimir's first kept memory:
  Juniper's first laugh, Bram's lullaby, or the False Dawn's light.
* **Endings.** He becomes Bram's opposite and best friend: one remembers
  everything, one is building a life from scratch.

### 20. Juniper Sleet: *the blank blade*
*Stalker · the Quiet (defector) · 19*
* **Before.** Born in the lower wards. At 15 she gave every memory she had to
  the Quiet's "Unburdening" rite and became their perfect assassin:
  fearless, empty, blindfolded.
* **During.** In Chapter 7 she tries to kill Maren and fails. Captured, she
  has no reason to refuse joining, or to do anything. Slowly she discovers
  that she likes things: tea, Lucien's hymns, winning.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 10. She asks Lucien to teach her a hymn "so I'll
  have something that's mine," and chooses to keep it forever.
* **Endings.** She finds her mother in the lower wards and starts a
  friendship with a stranger who is family (Long Telling). In the Silence
  ending she is the first to fall asleep, smiling.

### 21. Ruun Ashfather: *the chief who exiled his own*
*Vanguard · Ember-folk chief · 63*
* **Before.** Chief of the Ninth Fire and elder brother of **Ans**, who left
  at 17 to become a Lamplighter and is now Ansel Marrow. Ruun has never
  forgiven the lanterns for taking his brother. He exiled Sefa for selling
  the Telling, the second betrayal of his life.
* **During.** In Chapter 7 his tellers are losing verses and he comes to
  Candlemere with Kesh. He hears Sefa's confession. At the parley he sees
  his brother for the first time in 44 years.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 12. Ruun stands before Ansel and says their
  mother's name, which Ansel burned decades ago.
* **Endings.** He forgives Sefa and opens the Telling to the world, or
  refuses and leads his clan away. Either way, he brings his brother home
  as a story.

### 22. Old Kesh: *the teller with one story untold*
*Warden · Ember-folk · 84*
* **Before.** The Ninth Fire's eldest teller, blind for thirty years,
  great-aunt to Ruun and Ans. When Ans was 17 and grieving, she told him
  the story of Saint Idony and the lanterns. He left the next morning. She
  has blamed herself for 44 years.
* **During.** In Chapter 7 she tells Maren who Ansel really is. Her healing
  is the Telling itself: she heals by telling stories, in light or dark.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 12. She tells Ansel the story of his own childhood,
  which he burned long ago, and the Unlit Shepherd weeps.
* **Endings.** She dies in the epilogue having told every story she knew,
  surrounded by people telling hers.

### 23. Ilka of Myrr: *the bell-ringer of the forgotten*
*Arcanist · Unremembered (Myrr) · 340 (appears 40s)*
* **Before.** Princess of Myrr, daughter of King Oswy. When her father
  erased the 40,000 war-dead she refused, and climbed the bell-towers. She
  has rung one bell for each of them, every hour, for 312 years. As long as
  the bells ring, they are not entirely gone.
* **During.** In Chapter 8 her bells are heard by Nan. In Chapter 9 the
  company finds her. She guides them through Myrr and the truth of the
  Unmourning.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 11. She rings a new bell, made for Tobin, the first
  bell added in three centuries.
* **Endings.** She finally stops ringing, because now the world remembers,
  and rests (Long Telling). In the Silence ending she rings alone forever.

### 24. Tobin Vey: *the boy without a name*
*Blade · Unremembered · forgotten at 15*
* **Before.** Maren's little brother: funny, fearless, always carrying a tin
  toy lantern so he could "be a Lamplighter too". Nobody ever knew he was
  **Wick-born** like his sister. His memory burned so bright it held a
  causeway all night. On the Night of the Long
  Tide his sister burned her last memories of him to survive, and he faded
  out of the world. Ansel followed him into the dark and has protected him
  among the Unremembered ever since.
* **During.** In Chapter 1 he watches Maren from the dark and whispers
  *"Sister."* In Chapter 4 he holds Ansel's hand. In Chapter 7 he asks
  Kasimir if he knows his name. In Chapter 11 the company tries to restore
  him.
* **Key scene.** Chapter 11, if restored. The grey lifts from his face, he
  grins with a missing tooth and says, *"You don't remember me. That's
  okay. I remember you."*
* **Endings.** He becomes Maren's Teller (Long Telling, or the
  Lantern-Bearer if restored). If not restored, he is a boss Maren lays to
  rest without ever knowing him. The game never says this ending is wrong,
  only that it happened.

## 4. The antagonists' stories

### Ansel Marrow, the Unlit Shepherd (61)
* Born **Ans of the Ninth Fire**. He left the Ember-folk at 17 after the Hush
  took his mother, believing lanterns could save people where stories
  couldn't. He became the Order's greatest Lamplighter and burned nearly
  everything he was on the roads.
* On the Night of the Long Tide he watched his apprentice burn her brother
  to keep his lantern lit, and followed the boy into the dark. There he
  found peace among the forgotten, and founded **the Quiet**: if nothing is
  remembered, nothing can be burned.
* He keeps one memory unburned: Maren and Tobin laughing on the ferry the day
  he took her on.
* **Wants:** to end the cycle. **Wrong about:** the forgotten are not at peace.
* **His end:** he gives that last memory to the Long Telling and hears his
  childhood told back by Kesh. Or the world goes quiet beside him.

### High Witness Aurelie Sancroft (44)
* The Hush swallowed her parish when she was nine, and she still sleeps with
  three lamps lit. She has never burned a memory, believing she owes the
  Vigil a full heart.
* She read Idony's testament and believes the Sun-Altar is the exception,
  because it channels memory *into* the sun. The False Dawn seems to prove
  her right.
* **Wants:** daylight for everyone, paid for by the fewest. **Right about:**
  half-measures will fail.
* **Her end:** falters at her brother's lullaby (Long Telling), triumphs
  (Kindling), or is consumed by her own altar.

### Magnate Corvin Tallis (56)
* Elegant, dry, generous to his staff and ruthless to everyone else. He
  sells recall to the Church and funds the Quiet so that both need him. His
  endgame is a contract to sell daylight itself.
* He loves the daughter who sold every memory of him to escape him.
* **His end:** he turns his guards against the Church at the Altar if Noemi
  is with true friends; is ruined; or rules the century of light alone.

### The others
* **Dame Odile Brask** (Church Wick-Knight, Ch 6 boss): honourable, devout and
  wrong. She can surrender if Tamsin speaks to her.
* **King Oswy of Myrr** (Ch 10 boss): the man who invented forgetting as
  mercy. His shade still believes he was right.
* **The Ferryman** (Ch 1 boss): Greywater's founder, forgotten by the town he
  saved. He only wants his name.
* **Saint Idony Vash** (historical): she saved the world and knew the price.
  Her testament: *"I have taught them to eat the house to warm the hearth."*
* **Lantern-Marshal Corwen Ashby**: head of the Order and keeper of the Wick
  Purge secret. A decent coward.

## 5. Who to focus on first (unchanged)
**Maren, Bram, Sefa, Tamsin and Nox**, for four reasons:
1. They are the vertical slice (Chapter 1) and its playable squad.
2. They cover tank, ranged, healer and assassin, and all three light
   affinities.
3. They anchor the main factions.
4. The v2 interlocks make them the spine of the whole story. Bram remembers
   Tobin, Tamsin holds Maren's confession, Nox is Tallis's daughter, and
   Sefa's sold Telling connects to Ansel's family.

**Next:** Tobin, Isolde, Pell, Ines and the Nameless Knight.

## 6. Questions for you
1. **Tobin's unrestored path.** If the player burns too much, Maren must
   fight and lay Tobin to rest. Keep this hard ending, or always allow
   restoration (with a cost)?
2. **Ansel's redemption.** Ansel can be redeemed in the best ending. Keep
   that, or should he stay unredeemed?
3. **The Kindling ending.** It is the "dark" ending, where the villain's
   plan succeeds with Maren's help. Keep all four endings?
4. **Defined protagonist.** Maren is a fixed character with her own voice,
   not a blank avatar. Confirm?
5. **Tone check.** Pim's parents, Cass's wife and Vesper's lost love make
   memory loss painful on purpose. Is this the right level of darkness,
   given that the art will stay mostly cartoon?
6. **Maren's power level.** She is now the strongest unit at launch, always
   fielded, with a legendary weapon and an Act III awakening. Is that the
   "face of the game" level you want, or should she be stronger still (for
   example, the Naming awakening earlier, in Act II)?
7. **The Offering ending.** A new variant where Maren accepts the High
   Witness's offer and burns herself to spare 30,000. Keep it?
