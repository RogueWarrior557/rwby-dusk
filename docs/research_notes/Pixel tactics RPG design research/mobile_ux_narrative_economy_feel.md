Those items are marked **[primary, read]**. Most other hosts (uxmatters.com, shinkim.com, blog.colonist.io, GitHub API) were blocked by this session's network allowlist. The shared web-search budget also ran out partway through. Everything else therefore comes from **search-engine summaries of the linked pages** and is marked **[secondary, via search summary]**. ## 1. Mobile UX/UI: orientation, touch targets, reach, safe areas, legibility, accessibility, placement, density, navigation, battery

### Takeaway
Platform guidance gives firm numbers. Apple's default touch target is 44x44 pt (minimum 28x28 pt, for menus only), Google/Material's is 48x48 dp with 8 dp spacing, and Apple's minimum text size is 11 pt with a 17 pt default. Apple also requires layouts to respect safe areas and adapt to aspect ratios from 4:3 to 19.5:9, even in orientation-locked games. Choosing portrait or landscape is a real tradeoff: portrait favors one-handed, commute-style sessions (Clash Royale is the classic strategy example), and landscape favors battlefield width (Boom Beach). No neutral, quantitative market census of strategy/autobattler orientation was found.

### Cited Findings

#### Orientation (portrait vs landscape)
- **[practitioner opinion / developer blog, via search summary]** Colonist.io (an online Catan-like strategy board game) moved mobile to **portrait-first**. It claims "95% of mobile games today are portrait-first" and argues that one-handed play is the main benefit of committing to one orientation. The 95% figure has no traceable primary source and should not be treated as a market statistic. — [Colonist blog: Why Colonist.io is Going Portrait-First on Mobile Phones](https://blog.colonist.io/why-colonist-is-going-portrait-on-mobile)
- **[secondary, via search summary; URL attribution uncertain]** Supercell's **Clash Royale** (real-time strategy) is cited as designed for portrait and one-handed play. Supercell's earlier **Boom Beach** (strategy) is cited as designed for landscape. These examples appeared in results alongside [GameAnalytics on orientation](https://gameanalytics.com/?p=9503) and [UXtweak: Portrait vs Landscape](https://www.uxtweak.com/ux-glossary/portrait-vs-landscape). The specific attribution was not verified.
- **[practitioner opinion, via search summary]** Strategy games "need room for unit controls and battlefield views simultaneously," which favors landscape. Portrait developers should design for one-handed use because commuters on crowded trains can't rotate their phones. — [MobileMall Blog: How landscape gaming quietly won the format competition](https://mobilemall.co/blog/how-landscape-gaming-quietly-won-the-format-competition/)
- **[practitioner guidance, via search summary]** Unity's AR-design tutorial says supporting both orientations is ideal *if* the experience is actually enjoyable in both. The context is AR, so it's only a weak analogue. — [Unity Learn: Selecting an orientation](https://learn.unity.com/pathway/mobile-ar-development/unit/ar-experience-design/tutorial/selecting-an-orientation-for-your-ar-game)
- **[accessibility guideline, via search summary]** Game Accessibility Guidelines include the item "Allow play in both landscape and portrait." — [gameaccessibilityguidelines.com](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/allow-play-in-both-landscape-and-portrait/)
- **[trade press, title only]** PocketGamer.biz opinion piece "How portrait mode trumped tablet-first gaming." Only the title was retrieved, not the content. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/comment-and-opinion/62882/how-portrait-mode-trumped-tablet-first-gaming/)
- **[primary, read; Apple HIG Layout, change log updated 2026-09-09]** "Even if your app is locked to a certain orientation, such as a landscape-only game, it's still important to ensure your interface resizes well." Also: "Determine layout based on size classes, not device type or orientation." When aspect ratio varies, "don't change the aspect ratio of the artwork; instead, scale it so that it fills the screen completely… background artwork may often need to extend beyond what is typically visible." — [Apple HIG: Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- **[primary, read; Apple HIG Designing for games, change log 2025-06-09]** "Games need to look good and behave well at various aspect ratios, such as 16:10, 19.5:9, and 4:3". In-game menus must "remain legible and easy to use on every device — and, if you support them, in both orientations on iPhone and iPad". "Avoid fixed layouts as much as possible." — [Apple HIG: Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)

#### Touch targets
- **[primary, read; Apple HIG Accessibility, change log 2025-06-09]** For iOS/iPadOS, the **default control size is 44x44 pt and the minimum is 28x28 pt**. Spacing: "add about 12 points of padding around elements that include a bezel. For elements without a bezel, about 24 points of padding works well around the element's visible edges." — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[primary, read; Apple HIG Game controls, change log 2025-06-09]** "Make sure frequently used controls are a minimum size of 44x44 pt, and less important controls, such as menus, are a minimum size of 28x28 pt." — [Apple HIG: Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)
- **[primary platform doc, via search summary]** Android/Google recommends touch targets of **at least 48dp, separated by 8dp or more**, and 48dp is about **9 mm** regardless of screen size. — [Android Accessibility Help: Touch target size](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858). Material density guidance gives a default target of 48x48 dp with at least 8 dp between targets — [Material: Applying density](https://material.io/design/layout/applying-density.html). Jetpack Compose Material 3 components pad themselves to at least 48x48 dp, using `minimumInteractiveComponentSize()`. — [Android Developers: minimumInteractiveComponentSize](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/minimumInteractiveComponentSize.modifier)
- **[secondary, via search summary]** WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 2.5.5 (AAA) sets 44x44 CSS px, and Material's 48x48 dp is a platform guideline, not a conformance requirement. — [Craft CMS: Accessible target sizes](https://craftcms.com/blog/accessible-target-sizes)
- **[primary platform doc, via search summary]** A hit area can be larger than the drawn icon. For example, a 24x24 dp icon padded to a 48x48 dp target. — [Android Accessibility Help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)

#### Thumb-zone reachability
- **[primary research, via search summary; Feb 2013]** Steven Hoober observed people using phones in public settings: **1,333 observations**, reported as "over 1,300." About **49% held the phone one-handed**. One-handed users tapped with the thumb, and people often changed grip depending on the task. Hoober does not claim the study is scientific. — [UXmatters: How Do Users Really Hold Mobile Devices? (Hoober, 2013)](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)
- **[secondary, via search summary]** Hoober has argued that the commonly circulated thumb-zone heat-map is not correct as a universal standard, because reach varies with posture and device. Tablets tend to be held from the sides with both hands, with thumb input. — [Smashing Magazine: The Thumb Zone (2016)](https://smashingmagazine.com/2016/09/the-thumb-zone-designing-for-mobile-users); [A List Apart figure: tablet zones](https://alistapart.com/1-9-tablet-zones-jpg/)
- **[practitioner opinion, via search summary]** Josh Clark's estimate that about 75% of interactions are thumb-driven is often paired with Hoober's data. — [Smashing Magazine: How To Design Mobile Apps For One-Hand Usage (2020)](https://smashingmagazine.com/2020/02/design-mobile-apps-one-hand-usage/)
- **[primary, read; Apple HIG Game controls]** "Place frequently used buttons near a player's thumb… Place secondary controls, like menus, at the top of the screen." Also: "consider letting players tap objects to select them instead of adding a virtual selection button" (direct manipulation over virtual buttons). — [Apple HIG: Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)

#### Safe areas, notches, gesture bars, aspect ratios
- **[primary, read]** A safe area is "the area within a window that isn't covered on the edge by a hardware feature or another view… Respecting the safe area is essential to make sure system UI and hardware features like the Dynamic Island don't obstruct content and controls." — [Apple HIG: Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- **[primary, read]** "Make sure to position buttons where they don't overlap system features like the Home indicator or Dynamic Island on iPhone." — [Apple HIG: Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)
- **[primary, read]** "A device may have rounded corners or a camera housing… rely on platform-provided safe areas when possible." Prefer resolution-independent assets, or match game resolution to the device. — [Apple HIG: Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)

#### Small-screen text legibility
- **[primary, read]** iOS/iPadOS system text is **17 pt by default, with an 11 pt minimum**. Thin-weight custom fonts should run larger than recommended. Let people "enlarge text by at least 200 percent," either through custom UI or Dynamic Type. — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility). The same 17/11 pt table appears in game guidance, with the requirement that text "contrasts well with the background and uses at least the recommended minimum text size." — [Apple HIG: Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)
- **[primary, read]** Contrast minimums, which Apple takes from WCAG AA via Accessibility Inspector: **4.5:1 for text up to 17 pt; 3:1 for 18 pt and above, or for bold text**. Provide a higher-contrast scheme when the system Increase Contrast setting is on. — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[primary, read]** Apple links to its Unity plug-ins for supporting Dynamic Type (change log 2024-06-10). — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

#### Accessibility (color, motion, flashing, haptics)
- **[primary, read]** "Convey information with more than color alone… people who are color blind may have particular difficulty with pairings such as red-green and blue-orange… Consider allowing people to customize color schemes such as… game characters." — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[primary, read]** When Reduce Motion is on, "reduce automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion." Techniques include tightening springs, tracking animations to gestures, replacing x/y/z transitions with fades, and avoiding animated blurs. — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[primary, read; HIG Designing for games]** Let players "customize parameters like type size, game control mapping, motion intensity, and sound balance." Avoid relying solely on color. — [Apple HIG: Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)
- **[primary platform guideline, via search summary]** Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117: "Avoid the use of camera shake, camera bobbing effects, motion blur… or provide an option to turn off these behaviors." Halo Infinite is the cited example, with 0–100% sliders for screen shake, radial blur, full-screen effects, and speed lines. — [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117). XAG 118 says all games should be tested for photosensitive seizure triggers. — [XAG 118](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ie/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118)
- **[guideline, via search summary]** Game Accessibility Guidelines' basic level includes "avoid flickering images and repetitive patterns" and ensuring "no essential information is conveyed by a colour alone." — [Game Accessibility Guidelines: Basic](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/). Unity notes that flashing can also trigger migraines. — [Unity: Accessibility basics](https://unity.com/blog/video-game-accessibility-basics)
- **[primary, read; HIG Playing haptics, change log 2024-05-07]** Use haptics consistently, with a clear causal link to the action. Match the intensity and sharpness of a haptic to the animation it accompanies, and sync it with sound. "Avoid overusing haptics." "Let people turn off or mute haptics." Games may use custom, dynamic patterns, such as a stronger impact for a bigger hit. — [Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics). Pair audio cues with haptics for players who can't hear. — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

#### Drag-and-drop placement and touch feedback
- **[primary, read]** "Always include visible and tactile press states… adding a visual press state effect, such as a glow, that they can see even when their finger is covering the control. Combine this press state with sound and haptics." — [Apple HIG: Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)
- **[primary, read]** "Hide controls when an action isn't available or relevant… to reduce clutter." Use "touch and hold" and "double tap" to provide variations of an action instead of multi-button chords. — [Apple HIG: Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)
- **[primary, read]** "Let people cancel motion… don't make people wait for an animation to complete before they can do anything." — [Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)

#### Navigation (bottom-bar hubs)
- **[primary, read; HIG Tab bars, change log 2026-06-08]** On iOS, "a tab bar floats above content at the bottom of the screen". Use it "to support navigation, not to provide actions". Keep it visible across sections, except under modals. Reserve badges for critical information "so you don't dilute their impact". Include single-word labels and avoid overflow tabs. For user-customizable tabs, "aim for a default list of five or fewer." — [Apple HIG: Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)

#### Battery, thermal, frame-rate options
- **[primary, read; HIG Motion, change log 2025-09-09]** "In most games, maintaining a consistent frame rate of 30 to 60 fps typically results in a smooth, visually appealing experience." "Let people customize the visual experience of your game to optimize performance or battery life… consider letting people switch between power modes when the system detects the presence of an external power source." — [Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- **[primary, read]** Keep the initial download to 30 minutes or less and download extra content in the background. Use device information, such as resolution and accessibility settings, to pick defaults. — [Apple HIG: Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)

### Inferences
- **Orientation recommendation logic (my inference).** An autobattler's core loop is "prepare the board, then watch." That makes it unusually compatible with portrait: a tall grid fits naturally (bench and shop at the bottom in the thumb zone, enemy formation at the top), and the watch phase needs no input. Landscape mainly pays off for wide formations, simultaneous many-unit visibility, or a PC/Steam port. A practical hybrid is portrait on phones with a landscape or "wide" layout on tablets and PC, built on size classes as Apple recommends. It costs extra UI work, which the Unity and Game Accessibility Guidelines sources both acknowledge.
- **Battlefield sizing.** If the board must be fully visible in portrait at 360–430 pt widths, a 5–7 column grid gives roughly 50–80 pt cells. That is comfortably above the 44 pt target, so drag targets can stay one cell each without zoom. A wider landscape-style board (8+ columns) in portrait would push cells toward or below 44 pt. This is arithmetic, not a sourced finding.
- **Drag-and-drop placement.** The safest pattern combines the HIG items above. Make the drop target larger than the sprite (the hit area > visual principle from Android). Lift the dragged unit above the finger with a ghost or offset so the finger doesn't hide it, as with Apple's "visible even when their finger is covering the control". Snap to cell and preview the result: show the synergy or trait change before release. Always offer a tap-to-select, tap-to-place alternative for motor-impaired players and one-handed use (see Gaps on WCAG 2.5.7).
- **Pixel-art text.** Apple's 11 pt minimum and 4.5:1 contrast rule usually means pixel fonts must render at integer multiples of their native height. For example, an 8 px font at 2x or 3x in device pixels must still land at 11 pt or more. Keep body text in a clean, higher-resolution UI font, or offer a "readable font" toggle, so the art style doesn't override legibility. This is an inference from Apple's numbers.
- **Information density.** Use tooltips on long-press, not hover (there is no hover on touch), backed by a persistent unit-detail sheet. Hide inactive controls, as the HIG says. Badges must stay rare per the tab-bar guidance, or they turn into notification noise, which is common in hero RPG hubs.
- **Frame-rate options.** Offer a "30 fps / battery saver" vs "60 fps" toggle, and default to battery saver for long idle or auto-battle viewing. A pixel-art game gains little visually above 60 fps, and HIG says 30–60 fps is sufficient.

## 2. Story delivery in narrative-heavy mobile games: formats, skip/auto/log, recaps, archives, what players skip, tools

### Takeaway
The leading pattern in 2025–2026 is **"skippable but recoverable" story**. HoYoverse added paragraph-level Dialogue Skip that shows an auto-generated summary of what was skipped (Honkai: Star Rail 3.4), then added a Story Recap/replay feature. Arknights keeps optional depth in Operator Records and Archives, unlocked by progression and trust, and in permanent Event Records. No credible public telemetry on skip rates was found. Design for both audiences and instrument your own game. For a small team, **Ink (MIT)** and **Yarn Spinner (MIT core/Unity; YSPL for the Godot GDScript and Unreal versions)** are mature. **Dialogic 2 (MIT)** is Godot-native but still labeled alpha.

### Inferences
- **Recommended delivery stack (inference).** (1) Short main-chapter visual-novel scenes of about 2–5 minutes between battles, with Auto, Log/Backlog, and **paragraph-level Skip that shows a 1–3 line summary**, as HSR does. (2) A **"Previously on…" recap card** at chapter start. (3) Character bond stories unlocked by using a unit, as Arknights does with Trust, so combat-focused players earn lore passively and can read it whenever they like. (4) A **Codex/Archive** with replayable scenes (Story Recap) and lore entries. (5) Environmental storytelling in battle maps and unit idle animations, which costs no session time. Every story element should be skippable but recoverable.
- **The HSR history is a cautionary data point.** A narrative-first live game added skip and summary after launch, in response to demand. Building summaries into the content pipeline from day one is cheaper: author a one-line "beat summary" per scene in Ink or Yarn tags.

In Godot, Yarn Spinner's GDScript port uses the YSPL, so legal should review it. Dialogic is MIT but alpha, with breaking changes across Godot minor versions. Ink, through community Godot integrations, or Yarn's C# path avoids that risk. Yarn Spinner 3's storylets and saliency suit "which bond conversation is available now" systems.

## 3. Economy design and ethical monetization: faucets/sinks, curves, currencies, duplicates, reward schedules, regulation as of Nov 2026, fair models

### Takeaway
Regulation has converged on **mandatory odds disclosure everywhere you can sell on the major stores**: Apple since 2017, Google since 2019, China since 2017, and South Korea by statute since 22 March 2024, with punitive-damages litigation rules added in 2025. **Age-gating of paid random rewards** is spreading: the UK's industry principles (2023), Brazil (reported), and the **EU Digital Fairness Act proposal of 11 November 2026**, which would bar paid variable-reward mechanics for minors and require real-money price display across virtual-currency conversions. Belgium's de facto ban shows that bans without enforcement leave about 82% of top-grossing iPhone games still monetizing with randomness. For a new 2026–2027 title, the lowest-risk design avoids paid randomness (or at least discloses odds, pity, and per-item rates), uses one premium currency at most, shows real prices, and never ties a paid random set to a completion reward (Japan's kompu gacha ban).

### Inferences
- **Currency count (inference, anchored in regulation).** The DFA's requirement to show real-money value across successive conversions, plus Apple's rule that purchased currency can't expire, make multi-layer currency chains (cash → gems → tickets → shards) costly to comply with and look manipulative. Recommended: **at most one premium currency** that maps one-to-one to real prices in UI, **one soft currency** (gold) with clear sinks, and **item-specific materials** only where they are obviously "crafting." Show the real-money equivalent next to any premium price.
- **Duplicates (inference).** Korea's disclosure categories include **enhancement and synthesis** odds, not just gacha. So any randomized upgrade or fusion needs disclosed rates in Korea. Deterministic duplicate handling avoids that exposure and reads as fair: dupes convert to fixed shards or "memory fragments" that unlock bond story or cosmetic tiers, and shards buy any chosen unit (a "spark"). Avoid power-only duplicate stacking (e.g. +5 dupes = +100% stats); route dupes to sidegrades or story instead.
- **Pity/spark (inference).** If a gacha exists, GRAC's reported guidance on variable probability means the pity curve itself must be disclosed, not just base rates. Publish base rate, soft and hard pity thresholds, and the effective consolidated rate.
- **Kompu gacha avoidance (inference).** Never give a reward for completing a set of items that can be obtained only through paid random draws. A "collect all five Dusk Knights to unlock X" design is fine only if the units can be obtained deterministically.
- **Fair model for a narrative autobattler (inference).** The lowest-regulatory-risk and most review-friendly options: (1) **premium price**, or free demo plus one-time unlock; (2) **paid story expansions/chapters**; (3) **cosmetics sold directly** (skins, board themes, unit palettes), never randomized; (4) **no energy/stamina gating of story**, with optional daily bonuses instead; (5) direct-purchase unit packs at visible prices. This avoids DFA Article 15, Belgian gambling-law exposure, Korean probability-item obligations, and age-gating work entirely. If gacha is required commercially, use full odds plus pity disclosure, deterministic sparks, no minors' purchases (as the UK principles and the DFA proposal anticipate), and real-money pricing in UI.
- **Faucet/sink modeling (inference, standard practice).** Model each currency as a stock with explicit faucets (battle rewards, dailies, story chapter clears) and sinks (level-ups, bond gifts, cosmetics, re-rolls in the autobattler shop). Simulate daily net flow per player archetype (casual 10 min/day, core 45 min/day) in a spreadsheet before tuning. Use exponential costs for character levels with logistic caps at chapter boundaries, so progress gates align with story beats rather than paywalls.
- **Variable rewards ethics (inference).** Unpaid variable rewards, such as random battle loot from playing, sit outside paid-loot-box rules in every jurisdiction surveyed. Paid variable rewards are the regulated object. Keeping randomness on the gameplay side (autobattler shop rolls, battle drops) and money on the deterministic side is the cleanest ethical and legal split.

## 4. Game feel and audio: hit stop, screen shake, flashes, damage numbers, juice and its risks; audio layering, UI sounds, adaptive music, leitmotifs; SFX tools and asset licensing

### Takeaway

#### Trauma-based screen shake

#### Audio–haptic–visual coupling (UI sound conventions)
- **[primary, read]** Combine visual press states "with sound and haptics to enhance the feeling of feedback." — [Apple HIG: Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls). "Match the intensity and sharpness of a haptic with the intensity and sharpness of the animation it accompanies. You can also synchronize sound with haptics." Use consistent mappings, never the same pattern for success and failure. — [Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics). Pair audio cues ("success chime, error sound, or game feedback") with haptics for players who can't hear or have audio off. — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility). Let players adjust "sound balance". — [Apple HIG: Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)

### Inferences
- **Tactical-autobattler feel budget (inference).** Combat is mostly *watched*, not controlled, so juice should **clarify outcomes** more than reward inputs. Use hit stop of about 2–6 frames at 60 fps (about 33–100 ms) on crits, kills, and ultimates only, with a hard cap (Sakurai's rule), and apply it per unit (target and attacker) rather than freezing the whole board when many units fight at once. Use trauma-based shake for big events only (ultimates, boss phases), with a global shake slider defaulting to about 50–70% and honoring Reduce Motion. Use a white or palette-flash on hit for 1–2 frames, kept below full-screen flashes. Use damage numbers that stack or merge per unit, color-coded *and* shape- or icon-coded (crit glyph, element icon) to satisfy "not color alone." Offer a "Battle readability" preset: reduced particles, merged numbers, no shake.
- **Mobile thermals and juice.** Particle-heavy feedback multiplied by 10+ units and 2x/3x battle speed can spike GPU load. Tie particle density to the 30/60 fps or battery-saver setting.
- **Haptics.** Map light haptics to UI confirms, medium to unit placement snap, and heavy only to kills or ultimates. Follow Apple's consistency rule (don't reuse the "defeat" pattern for "victory") and provide a mute toggle.
- **Audio design (inference, standard practice, unsourced here).** Layer impact sounds as transient (click or snap) + body (thud or tonal) + tail (debris or reverb). Vary pitch ±3–5% and round-robin samples so repeated autobattle hits don't fatigue the ear. Duck music under ultimates. UI sounds should form one consistent family: short, mid-frequency, distinct for confirm, back, error, and purchase. Adaptive music works well as vertical layering in combat (add percussion or brass as the board state escalates) and horizontal re-sequencing for phase changes. Narrative leitmotifs per faction or character, reused in bond scenes and boss themes, tie the story to combat cheaply. Undertale-style motif reuse is the commonly cited indie exemplar, but it is unverified here.
- **SFX pipeline for a small team.** Prototype in ChipTone (CC0 output) or jfxr. Finalize by layering in a DAW. Source only CC0 (Kenney, CC0-filtered Freesound) to avoid attribution tracking, and keep a license ledger anyway.
