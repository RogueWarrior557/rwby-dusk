## 1. Modes: what exists, how each works, and how they fit the loop

### Takeaway
KGC is built around one deck of heroes that is reused across many short, gated modes. The **Invasion** chapters are the main 20-round PvE progression and also the template for other modes. Around them sit:
- a roguelike **Great Rift**
- asynchronous **Arena** (saved defense lineups) and a newer real-time **Battlefield** PvP mode
- weekly-staggered seasonal content (**Tower of Trials** in week 2, **Alliance Raid** in week 3)
- resource side modes (**Expedition**)
- a base-building meta layer (**Dominion**)
- a story mode, rebuilt in 2026 (Story Chapter 2 announced June 2026)

Almost every mode pays out the same few shared currencies, mainly Gold, Castle EXP, pass points and tokens. Each mode is a different "lens" on the same collection rather than a separate game.

### Inferences
- **Season cadence is about 4 weeks.** Season 56 was in May 2025 and Season 75 in November 2026, which is 19 seasons in about 18 months. The build numbers track this (161 in November 2025, 168 in May 2026, 175 in November 2026). Week-staggered openings (Trials in week 2, Alliance Raid in week 3 for 10 days) give each week of a season its own "new thing". This is a cheap, strong live-ops rhythm worth copying in spirit.
- **Mode reuse is the main content-cost lesson.** Arena team setup *replays specific Invasion floors* (1/5/10/15/20). Tower of Trials remixes Invasion with roster removal. Corruption is a harder Invasion at 2× cost for about 1.6× reward. This multiplies content from one set of encounter data. Corruption's reward ratio (about 1.6× payout for 2× entry cost) deliberately makes hard mode *less efficient per stamina* but faster per minute, and it adds exclusive tokens.
- **The Great Rift has the classic "roguelike layer over a gacha collection" structure.** Your permanent collection forms the deck; run-only relics and altar cap raises (15 → up to 25) give within-run power. A stamina gate (5 per floor) caps how often you can run it. Players say the stamina gate clashes with roguelike replay, which is a design warning.
- **Pay-for-loss is avoided in Invasion.** Rewards are granted win or lose. This softens fail states in a stamina-gated game.
- **Arena is the async-PvP-vs-AI-snapshot pattern.** Real-time PvP was added only three years in, as a separate mode. For a small team, async snapshot PvP is the low-risk default.

## 2. Progression and economy: currencies, permanent vs. temporary upgrades, gates and walls

### Takeaway
KGC runs a **many-currency, many-gate** economy:
- **Supplies ("meat")** are the stamina for most PvE (Invasion, Great Rift).
- **Mode-specific tickets** gate the side modes: Flags of Trials, Expedition Flags.
- **Castle Level** (from Castle EXP) is the account-wide unlock spine: Expedition at CL5, Relics at CL7, Arena at CL10, Legacies at CL15, altar cap growth up to 25. Castle Level rewards reportedly stop after CL30 except cosmetics.

Permanent power stacks across heroes (summon/merge), **Relics** (4 tiers plus sub-stat reforging), **Legacies** (per-hero items, level 10 → transcend to 30), the **Altar**, **Dominion** buildings and **Noble Phantasms** (a gacha equipment layer). The main walls players complain about are stamina scarcity for the Great Rift and the gacha odds of new heroes falling as the roster grows.

- "The odds of getting important heroes keep falling as the roster grows" (criticism page). — [Namu (EN mirror): Criticism and Controversy](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%ED%82%B9%EA%B0%93%EC%BA%90%EC%8A%AC/%EB%B9%84%ED%8C%90%20%EB%B0%8F%20%EB%85%BC%EB%9E%80)

### Inferences
- **Collection dilution is the structural risk of a growing roster.** With a flat "chance of a *new* hero" model, each added hero lowers the odds of a specific one. Rate-ups (50% featured) and a direct-purchase Daily Shop (149 gems) are the pressure valves. For an original design, prefer deterministic unlocks or pity/shard systems so that adding content does not lower odds for older players.
- **Stamina conflicts with roguelike modes.** A 9-floor run at 5 Supplies per floor, plus failures, costs most of a day's stamina. Separate roguelike entry (weekly tickets, or free runs with capped rewards) from the main stamina pool.
- **One account level as the unlock spine (Castle Level) is clean.** Rewards drying up after CL30 except cosmetics is a classic late-game motivation cliff. Plan the long tail (prestige or seasonal tracks) explicitly.
- **Equipment is layered three deep:** Relics (account/altar), Legacies (per hero) and Noble Phantasms (gacha). Each layer adds a reroll or transcend sink. This yields long-term goals but also the "P2W" perception (see §3).

- Exact daily Supplies regen, cap and refill costs; daily/weekly mission structure; ad-reward list beyond the daily free summon.
- Current, official summon rates, including Noble Phantasm rates and any pity system. No official probability page was found (see §3).
- Hero upgrade/merge cost tables (the other researcher covers merge mechanics).
- What "Smelt" rounds do, and what the Altar choices are in standard Invasion.

### Inferences
- **Time-limited ad removal bundled into a seasonal pass is a trust hazard.** Players experience ad removal as a permanent entitlement. If ads exist, sell permanent ad removal separately, or make it clear on the purchase screen that it is seasonal.

- **Reworking existing paid or invested power** (a relic option rework, a hero nerf tied to new paid gear) caused KGC's worst backlash. Protect existing player investment, and offer refunds or resets when you rebalance.
- **Reselling "limited" cosmetics breaks scarcity promises.** If you sell limited items, define "limited" and keep the promise.

## 4. Reward psychology: what keeps players playing, what makes them quit

### Takeaway
Players stay for the **merge-and-build satisfaction** of tower defense crossed with auto-chess, for **collecting heroes**, for **events**, and for a long ladder of permanent upgrades with seasonal resets that always offer a next goal. They quit over **grind and repetition**, **stamina limits**, **falling gacha odds as the roster grows**, **ads that come back after a paid season**, and **trust-breaking changes**: reworks of invested power, a lenient punishment for a bug exploit, and a limited-skin resale.

### Cited Findings
**Keeps them playing**
- "Merging games with cool characters and design"; "a tower defense game mixed with auto chess which just works and is fun to play"; praise for events. — [AppGrooves digest](https://appgrooves.com/android/com.awesomepiece.castle/kinggodcastle/awesomepiece/positive); [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/king-god-castle/id1526791430)

- Invasion rewards come win or lose, plus pass points. — [KGC Fandom: Invasion](https://kgc.fandom.com/wiki/Invasion)

### Inferences
- **Win-or-lose rewards plus pass points** reduce frustration in hard content and keep daily sessions feeling productive.
- **Week-staggered seasonal unlocks** (Trials in week 2, Raid in week 3) create appointment play without daily-login guilt. A good model for an offline or premium game is "chapters" or "weekly challenge rotations".
- The quit drivers are mostly **economy and trust**, not core gameplay. The core loop is praised; the friction comes from gates and monetization choices. For an original premium or fair-F2P design, the opportunity is to keep the "merge/place/watch" satisfaction and remove stamina and odds dilution.

### Inferences
These inferences rest on genre practice, not on verified KGC specifics.

**Generic genre practice (safe to learn from):**
- small chibi-proportioned pixel units on a readable grid
- rank escalation of the same enemy family (grunt → General → King) to make elites and bosses readable through palette or scale variants
- round-number pacing: elite every 5th round, boss at 20
- floating damage numbers and hit flashes
- tiered rarity color-coding (bronze → gold → prismatic)
- bottom- or corner-tab hub navigation on mobile
- seasonal cosmetic skins

All of these are standard across merge-defense, auto-battler and tower-defense games, and none is unique to KGC.

**Likely distinctive to KGC (do not copy):**
- the "King / God / King God" tier vocabulary and the 킹갓 slang branding
- the specific castle-defense framing (the king's castle being invaded)
- its named enemy families (e.g., "Lime" slimes with General/King ranks)
- the "Incarnate" boss roster, the "Noble Phantasm" equipment naming, and "Luminous Fate" story branding
- its hero designs and art

An original game should invent its own tier names, enemy families and framing device.

**A useful structural lesson:** using **one escalation vocabulary across every system** (relic tiers, chest grades, difficulty names) builds instant legibility and brand. Copy the *technique* with your own vocabulary.
